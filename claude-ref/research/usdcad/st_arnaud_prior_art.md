# St-Arnaud prior art: the CAD–oil correlation breakdown mechanism

Research date: 2026-06-05. Researcher: macro-research-department research economist.

Question: Has Charles St-Arnaud (chief economist, Alberta Central; ex-BoC, Morgan
Stanley, Nomura FX) PUBLISHED the specific mechanism — petrocurrency pre-2014 →
post-2014 capital discipline → shareholder returns (dividends/buybacks) → foreign
ownership of the shareholder base → oil USD revenue no longer converted to CAD →
structural break in the CAD–oil link?

**Bottom line up front: yes, in full, under his name, repeatedly. This is not an
unpublished verbal idea. St-Arnaud has put every link of the chain in print across
at least three venues since December 2022, and it has been picked up in tier-1
media (Bloomberg/Financial Post, April 2026). The full conversion-mechanism framing
is his signature contribution; he is the originator of record for this specific
chain in the Canadian context.**

---

## 1. St-Arnaud's published statements of the mechanism

### Source A (canonical, full chain) — Alberta Central, "The Canadian dollar: A petro-currency no more"
- Venue: Alberta Central, Intelligence Centre / Economic News
- URL: https://albertacentral.com/intelligence-centre/economic-news/the-canadian-dollar-a-petro-currency-no-more/
- Date: ~2023 (page renders relative timestamp "2 years ago" as of 2026-06; no
  hard datestamp surfaced via fetch — **date needs confirmation if used as a
  citation**; it post-dates the Dec 2022 C.D. Howe memo and shares its data).
- Author: Charles St-Arnaud (Alberta Central chief economist; byline not rendered
  in the fetched body but the analysis is his and the series is his).

This is the most complete published statement of the exact mechanism. All five
links are present:

1. **Petrocurrency pre-2014 (with econometrics):** "from 1997 to 2014, the
   coefficient associated with energy commodity prices is significant" — i.e. the
   CAD historically moved with energy prices; "Historically, both moved closely to
   each other."
2. **Capital discipline / reduced reinvestment:** oil producers reinvest "about 9%
   of their revenues ... over the past year. This compares to about 25% of revenues
   in 2014."
3. **Shareholder returns up:** "about 10% of revenues (almost $20bn over the past
   year) are returned to shareholders through share buybacks and dividends. In
   comparison, that proportion was about 3% of revenues ... in 2014."
4. **Foreign ownership of the base:** "about 78% of these shareholders are
   non-Canadian, compared to 62% in 2014." (Outflow to foreign holders quantified
   elsewhere in his work at ~1.5% of GDP, up from ~0.4%.)
5. **USD→CAD conversion mechanism (the load-bearing link):** "Most oil producers'
   cash is likely held in USD ... only the revenues converted into CAD to pay for
   expenses in Canada will affect the exchange rate."

Note: the conversion link is stated in two adjacent sentences rather than one
compound sentence. The logical chain (less reinvestment + outflows to foreigners
→ less USD converted to CAD → weaker support for the loonie) is unambiguously his
argument, assembled across the piece.

### Source B (academic-tier, co-authored) — C.D. Howe Institute, "The Oilpatch is Gushing Money. Where's the Boom?"
- Authors: Glen Hodgson (C.D. Howe fellow-in-residence) and Charles St-Arnaud
- Venue: C.D. Howe Institute, Intelligence Memos
- URL: https://www.cdhowe.org/intelligence-memos/oilpatch-gushing-money-wheres-boom
  (also at https://cdhowe.org/publication/oilpatch-gushing-money-wheres-boom/)
- Date: **December 19, 2022**
- This is the citable, institution-backed version. It frames the same data
  (reinvestment collapse, shareholder returns, foreign ownership) and explicitly
  carries the implication through to "the value of the loonie" and Canada's
  capital flows. The Alberta Central "petro-currency no more" piece is the
  currency-focused expansion of this memo's argument.
- A related C.D. Howe / Globe and Mail op-ed exists: "Where's the oil boom? Why
  Alberta's recent good fortune is mostly a mirage."

### Source C (media pickup, tier-1, 2026) — Bloomberg / Financial Post
- Article: "Canadian Dollar's Weakening Ties to Oil Prices Amplifies Bets Against It"
- Authors: Anya Andrianova and Erik Hertzberg (Bloomberg)
- Date: April 22, 2026 (republished via EnergyNow; same wire ran in Financial Post
  "Posthaste" and Canadian Mortgage Trends)
- URL: https://energynow.ca/2026/04/canadian-dollars-weakening-ties-to-oil-prices-amplifies-bets-against-it/
- St-Arnaud quoted directly: "Canadian companies are returning a big portion of
  their revenue to shareholders, where 75 per cent of those are not Canadians,
  meaning a smaller share of oil revenue is being invested back into operation and
  domestic economy, diminishing demand for the local currency." Notes the
  decoupling started "as early as 2016."

### Supporting — CBC News, oil industry capital-return / "mature phase"
- "Oil industry continues focus on returning cash to investors over new big projects"
- URL: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/alberta-jackie-forrest-charles-st-arnaud-oil-and-gas-1.7584486
- St-Arnaud on the "mature phase": companies "making a very good return on those
  investments ... don't see the need to expand dramatically." This is the
  capital-discipline leg in his own words, in mainstream media.

|---|---|---|

## 3. Other prior art on the same mechanism

The **shock-source** explanation is a genuinely DIFFERENT, competing mechanism —
worth distinguishing in any writeup:

- **Scotiabank Economics — Olivier Gervais, "Understanding the CAD–Oil Decoupling:
  The Source of the Oil Shock Matters," April 16, 2026.**
  URL: https://www.scotiabank.com/ca/en/about/economics/economics-publications/post.other-publications.insights-views.cad-and-oil-decoupling--april-16--2026-.html
  Argues the apparent decoupling is largely a **composition-of-shocks** artifact:
  CAD responds more to demand-driven oil moves than supply-driven ones; controlling
  for shock source + rate differentials + USD, "the apparent decoupling does not
  require a breakdown in economic relationships." This is the rival framing to
  St-Arnaud's structural-flows story. (Sell-side; usable as a contrasting view to
  characterize, not as primary authority per house rules.)

- **Bank of Canada** — long-standing commodity-price exchange-rate modelling (e.g.
  Staff Analytical Note 2017-1, "The Share of Systematic Variations in the Canadian
  Dollar—Part II," Feb 2017). BoC documents the post-2017 weakening of the energy
  coefficient but, in the notes surfaced, does NOT advance the
  dividend/buyback/foreign-ownership/USD-conversion causal chain. BoC = the
  empirical decoupling; St-Arnaud = the flows-based causal story. Worth a targeted
  follow-up search of more recent BoC SANs before asserting BoC silence on the
  mechanism.

- General energy-sector "capital discipline" framing (Finimize, QV Investors, trade
  press) is widespread post-2020, but as an equity/industry narrative — NOT linked
  to the CAD exchange-rate-conversion mechanism. The currency bridge is what
  distinguishes St-Arnaud.

# Gold-beta prior art (verified 2026-06-05)

Question for this section: has anyone tested whether the Canadian dollar picked
up a GOLD beta — i.e., (a) that CAD now responds to gold prices, (b) tested
empirically with correlations/betas, and (c) the generalized ownership-structure
thesis (export-currency link severed by foreign ownership + payout discipline)
applied to gold rather than oil?

**Bottom line up front: the empirical gold-CAD question has been published — the
"CAD now responds to gold" claim and a quantified explanatory-power test both
exist, and the canonical citation is Desjardins (Mirza Shaheryar Baig), 26 Sep
2025. The generalized ownership-structure thesis applied to GOLD has NOT been
published by anyone found. St-Arnaud's flows mechanism remains oil-only; nobody
has carried it across to gold miners. That carry-across is the open lane for
Sibley Creek.**

## (a) Relevant publications found

### G1 — Desjardins, "Gold and the Loonie" — THE canonical gold-beta prior art
- Author: **Mirza Shaheryar Baig**, Foreign Exchange Strategist, Desjardins
- Venue: Desjardins Economic Studies
- Date: **September 26, 2025**
- URL: https://www.desjardins.com/en/savings-investment/economic-studies/canada-gold-loonie-26-september-2025.html
- How far it goes: **Furthest of anyone on the empirical question.** Runs a factor
  decomposition of weekly USDCAD variance and reports the headline result that
  **"gold prices currently explain about 12% of the weekly variance in USDCAD —
  far more than oil prices, but a distant runner-up to monetary policy, which
  explains roughly 25%."** Explicitly establishes that gold > oil as a CAD driver
  now and that gold's influence is rising. Discusses gold exports (precious-metals
  share of total exports ~3.5% doubling toward ~7% in 2025) and gold miners as
  ~12% of TSX market cap. Frames the puzzle ("why hasn't the loonie risen with
  gold?") and resolves it with **rate differentials** — "the loonie isn't ignoring
  gold prices, but their impact is being offset by interest rate expectations."
  - Crucial gap: does **NOT** invoke ownership structure, foreign ownership of
    miners, dividend/buyback payout, or USD-revenue-not-converted-to-CAD. The
    offset is monetary policy, not a structural flows break. So Baig owns the
    *empirical beta* but not the *structural mechanism*.

### G2 — Investment Executive, "Loonie not yet glittering, despite gold surge"
- Author: James Langton. Venue: Investment Executive. Date: September 26, 2025
- URL: https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/research-and-markets/loonie-not-yet-glittering-despite-gold-surge/
- How far it goes: Trade-press writeup OF the Desjardins G1 note; same 12%/25%
  figures, same rate-differential offset. Adds the Desjardins line that commodity
  support could firm the loonie in 2026 if "higher prices and shifts in government
  policy stimulate domestic production." No independent analysis; no ownership
  mechanism. Useful only as a secondary pointer to G1.

### G3 — RBC Economics, "Surging gold prices ... cushion Canada's exports"
- Venue: RBC Economics. URL: https://www.rbc.com/en/economics/canadian-analysis/featured-analysis/insights/surging-gold-prices-inroads-to-foreign-markets-cushion-canadas-exports/
- How far it goes: Connects the gold-price surge to the **trade balance** (gold as
  Canada's #2 export after crude; UK-destined gold +~76% / +$17bn in 2025), not to
  the **exchange rate**. Trade-flow story, no FX beta, no mechanism. Sell-side;
  input not citation.

### G4 — FXStreet, "What really moves the Canadian Loonie now?"
- Author: Joshua Gibson. Date: June 1, 2026. URL: https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/recession-on-paper-what-really-moves-the-canadian-loonie-now-202606011936
- How far it goes: Treats gold only as a Q1-GDP **import** accounting artifact, not
  as a CAD driver. Argues oil terms-of-trade + BoC/Fed rate differentials drive CAD.
  No gold beta, no ownership mechanism. Effectively negative evidence — a June 2026
  FX commentary that does NOT pick up the gold-beta angle.

### G5 — Bank of Canada exchange-rate modelling (negative evidence)
- Staff Analytical Note 2025-2, "Monetary policy, interest rates and the Canadian
  dollar," Feb 2025 (Fontaine, Krohn, Kyeong, Vala, Zmitrowicz).
  URL: https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2025/02/staff-analytical-note-2025-2/
- How far it goes: CAD-driver regression uses **WTI crude only**; gold is absent
  as a regressor. No foreign-ownership / dividend / USD-conversion mechanism. BoC
  has not added a gold leg to its published FX framework. (Also checked: SAN 2024-20,
  2025-14/16, Jan-2025 MPR "Recent factors affecting the exchange rate" — none
  surface a gold-CAD beta.) BoC = empirical FX modelling without gold; confirms the
  gold beta is not yet in the official-sector literature.

### G6 — "Dutch Disease" framing in trade/mining commentary (tangential)
- Discovery Alert / trade-press pieces note that surging precious-metals exports
  could, in principle, appreciate CAD and hurt manufacturing competitiveness
  (Dutch-Disease analogy). This is the *opposite* of the ownership-break thesis:
  it assumes the export boom DOES flow into CAD. Non-rigorous, no data test, no
  ownership mechanism. Listed for completeness; it actually motivates the puzzle
  Sibley would resolve.

## (b) Three verdicts

**[1] "CAD now responds to gold prices" — PUBLISHED.** Desjardins/Baig (26 Sep
2025) states it explicitly and quantifies it: gold explains ~12% of weekly USDCAD
variance, more than oil. Trade press (Investment Executive) echoed it the same day.
This is established prior art; Sibley cannot claim novelty on the existence of a
gold-CAD relationship.

**[2] "gold-CAD link tested empirically with correlations/betas" — PUBLISHED
(partially).** Baig ran a factor/variance decomposition (12% of weekly USDCAD
variance attributable to gold; ~25% to monetary policy). That is an explanatory-
power test, not a reported single beta coefficient or correlation table — he does
NOT publish a gold beta number, a correlation coefficient, or rolling-window
regression output. So the *claim that it has been tested* is published, but a
**clean published gold beta / rolling-correlation series is NOT on the record.**
Open lane: a properly specified rolling gold beta on dlog(CAD) with the standard
controls (rate diff, USD broad index, oil) is Sibley-original if built. Baig's 12%
is the benchmark to beat / cite against, not a substitute.

**[3] "the generalized ownership-structure thesis applied to gold" — NOT
PUBLISHED.** No source found carries St-Arnaud's foreign-ownership + payout-
discipline + USD-revenue-not-converted mechanism across from oil to gold miners.
Baig's offset is rate differentials, not a flows break. St-Arnaud's published work
is oil-patch-specific and does not mention gold producers. The generalized claim —
"the export-currency link is severed by ownership/payout structure, not by the
commodity, and gold is the clean second test case" — appears to be genuinely
**unoccupied**. That is the defensible Sibley contribution, provided it is built on
gold-miner ownership/payout data (foreign-ownership share, dividend/buyback ratios,
USD cash holdings) rather than asserted by analogy.

## (c) Implication for the Sibley piece
- The "nobody has tested whether CAD picked up a gold beta" claim as originally
  phrased is **too strong** — Baig tested it and found ~12% explanatory power.
  Reframe to: the *existence* of a gold-CAD link is documented (Desjardins), but
  **the structural mechanism — why a gold-export boom does or doesn't convert into
  CAD demand — has not been examined for gold.** The generalization of the
  ownership/payout thesis beyond oil is the novel leg.
- If used, Baig (Desjardins) must be cited as the prior art on the empirical
  gold-CAD relationship and the 12%/25% figures attributed to him, exactly as
  St-Arnaud is cited for the oil mechanism. Treat both as sell-side INPUTS to
  characterize, not as primary authority; rebuild the beta and the ownership/payout
  numbers from primary data (StatCan trade + FX, BoC Valet, gold-miner filings /
  shareholder registries) before any number ships as Sibley-derived.
