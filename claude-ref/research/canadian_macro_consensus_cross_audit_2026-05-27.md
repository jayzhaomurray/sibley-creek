## Referee Synthesis

The Codex memo is a competent forecast-consensus scaffold. It is strongest on
published medians: GDP, CPI, BoC rate path, unemployment, CAD, and the basic
"weak but not recessionary" macro shape.

The Claude pass is stronger on product strategy. It names the Canadian-specific
regime themes the Codex memo underweighted: population reversal, per-capita vs
aggregate GDP, mortgage renewals, tariff pass-through, export-diversification
rhetoric, internal-trade rhetoric, GoC term-premium / fiscal-supply risk, and
the BoC's scenario-communication problem.

The combined answer is:

> The Canadian macro consensus is not just "slow growth and BoC hold." It is a
> managed-adjustment story built on three assumptions: trade shock stays
> manageable, inflation returns to target because slack does the work, and
> Canada can bridge weak private-sector investment with public/infrastructure
> policy, population normalization, and eventual productivity repair.

That consensus is directionally coherent but soft on measurement. The opening
for Sibley Creek is to attack the measurement gaps, not merely to take hotter
macro views.

## What Survives

### 1. Weak Growth, Not Recession

This survives. The forecast-center is weak growth in 2026 with modest recovery
in 2027. Bank of Canada MPS, Department of Finance private-sector survey, BoC
MPR, and bank forecasts all live in this zone.

Use as baseline, not wedge.

### 2. Excess Supply Contains Inflation

This survives. The negative output-gap view is broad, and the BoC/market
participant survey evidence is strong. But the inflation framing needs to be
Canadian-specific: oil shock, tariff risk, weak demand, and carbon-price/base
effects interact. Do not reduce it to "energy lift then target."

### 3. BoC Hold Is Survey Consensus, Not The Whole Market Consensus

This survives with a correction. The median survey path says hold through 2026,
then gradual rises in 2027. But that is not the same thing as live market
pricing, and it is not the same thing as BoC scenario communication.

Product implication: the valuable object is a **three-way BoC path tracker**:
market-implied path vs economist/survey median vs BoC scenario signal.

### 4. Productivity / Business Investment Is The Structural Core

This survives, but the first memo overstated the degree to which "repair" is
consensus. The safer claim:

> Everyone agrees productivity and business investment are the problem. The
> disagreement is whether any existing policy or market mechanism credibly fixes
> it.

This is a strong Sibley Creek lane if measured through investment, intangibles,
R&D, patents, capital deepening, and profit-generation consequences.

### 5. Trade Uncertainty Is Consensus But Stale

This survives. Everyone knows trade uncertainty matters. Sibley Creek should
not write "trade uncertainty is bad." The useful work is mechanism-specific:
which export shares changed, which investment decisions moved, which tariff
pass-through shows up, and where diversification rhetoric is not visible in
data.

## What Gets Demoted

### "Canada Has No AI Landscape"

Promising but not yet publishable as a central claim. It needs evidence:
AI capex, data-centre investment, listed-company exposure, software/IP
formation, patent/R&D metrics, cloud adoption, and the TSX earnings link.

Treat it as a research lane, not a settled wedge.

### "Retail Sales Is Not Consumption"

Useful but probably not a high-end institutional wedge by itself. Bank
economists know this; the weaker target is popular commentary and market
overreaction to monthly retail prints.

Better version: **Canadian consumption tracker: retail, services, real income,
population, SEPH payroll income, and national-accounts consumption.**

### "Cheap Canadian Equities Are Not A Thesis"

Directionally useful, but it drifts into asset-allocation strategy unless tied
to macro profit mechanics. Do not make broad equity calls. Make the macro
claim: private investment, public-sector share, AI absence, sector mix, and
productivity shape Canada's profit-growth capacity.

## Missing Themes Added By Cross-Audit

1. Population reversal / immigration-policy shock.
2. Aggregate vs per-capita GDP convergence.
3. Mortgage-renewal wall by vintage and cash-flow channel.
4. Carbon-price removal / energy / tariff pass-through interaction in CPI.
5. GoC supply, fiscal deficits, defense spending, and term-premium risk.
6. Internal-trade liberalization rhetoric vs measurable gains.
7. Export-diversification rhetoric vs sticky trade concentration.
8. BoC scenario-based communication vs point-forecast consensus.
9. Bimodal tariff/CUSMA distribution rather than smooth forecast medians.

## Corrected Wedge Ranking

### Tier 1: Build These First

1. **Three-way BoC path tracker**
   - Market-implied path vs survey/economist median vs BoC scenario signal.
   - Directly answers Fred's "not next meeting; 6-12 months" point.

2. **Productivity / business-investment repair test**
   - The street agrees this is the problem but does not test the repair
     mechanism rigorously.

3. **Per-capita Canada tracker**
   - Aggregate GDP, GDP per capita, labour force, population, hours, and income.
   - Canada-native, repeatable, hard to fake.

4. **Tariff / CPI pass-through tracker**
   - Component-level CPI and tariff-sensitive baskets.
   - Turns a repeated claim into a measured framework.

### Tier 2: Good Follow-Ups

5. **Diversification reality check**
   - Export-destination and import-source shares vs policy rhetoric.

6. **Mortgage-renewal wall by vintage**
   - Good recurring Canadian macro channel, especially for consumption and bank
     credit risk.

7. **Consumption construction note**
   - Retail vs services vs real income vs SEPH/national accounts.

### Tier 3: Research Before Productizing

8. **Canada AI absence**
   - Needs hard evidence and clean market linkage.

9. **Cheap equities are not a thesis**
   - Needs a macro-profit framework or it becomes generic asset allocation.

## Decision

Do not use the first Codex memo alone for planning. Use it as the forecast
scaffold. Use Claude's pass for missing theme coverage. Use this cross-audit as
the corrected planning basis.

Next product-planning move: choose one Tier 1 wedge and define the recurring
artifact format, data inputs, and first 500-word note.
