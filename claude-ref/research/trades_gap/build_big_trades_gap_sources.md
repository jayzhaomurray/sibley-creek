### 2026-05-26 — RBC Economics (Janzen + Zheng): "Canada's looming labour squeeze: The impact of retirements and immigration policy"
- URL: https://www.rbc.com/en/economics/canadian-analysis/featured-analysis/insights/canadas-looming-labour-squeeze-the-impact-of-retirements-and-immigration-policy/
- Date: 2026-05-26 (published today)
- Authors: Nathan Janzen (Assistant Chief Economist), Annie Zheng (Economist)
- Key argument: structural labour supply decline due to record retirements + immigration restrictions. Population shrinks in 2026 for the first time on record. Workforce participation declining. Labour shortages will emerge across sectors despite current elevated unemployment.
- Key numbers cited:
  - Monthly retirements ~25,500 (0.12% of labour force) — nearly double the ~14,000/month two decades ago
  - Immigrant workforce share: 28.4% of Canada's workforce
  - Under-35 population fell 120,500 over one year (to April)
  - Projected under-35 workforce decline ~139,000/yr over next 5 years without immigration
  - Temporary resident cap target ~5% of total population by mid-2027
  - 17% of CFIB firms report unskilled/semi-skilled shortages
- **Does NOT engage Build Canada / MPO project portfolio as a labour-demand driver.**
- **Does NOT analyze skilled trades specifically.** Discusses labour broadly with un/semi-skilled mentions.
- Policy implication: immigration caps may need easing once targets are met to offset structural labour supply headwinds.
- **Relevance for Sibley's piece:** the supply-side complement. RBC establishes structural labour-supply decline; Sibley adds the demand-side Build-Big overlay RBC didn't do, specifically for skilled trades. Cite explicitly. Sent to Jay by William Dahmer 2026-05-26.

### Spring Economic Update 2026, Chapter 2 — key Team Canada Strong text (primary source)

**Most consequential quote — the government concedes Build Canada adds to the labour need:**

> "retirements and economic growth mean that Canada will need more than 1.4 million additional trades workers by 2033, AND THE SCALE OF INVESTMENTS IN HOUSING AND INFRASTRUCTURE PROJECTS WILL ADD TO THIS DEMAND."

**Target and timeline:**
- 80,000-100,000 new Red Seal trades workers by 2030-31 (5-year window)
- "If nothing changes, Canada will face a persistent gap of more than 20,000 skilled trades workers per year"
- Federal program rate: ~16-20K/year — matches the structural-gap rate, zero headroom for Build Canada incremental

**Apprenticeship completion data (2024):**
- 100,000+ new apprentices registered
- ~34,000 completed
- Federal program targets completion specifically (largest bucket is the $3.4B "Hire" portion with completion bonuses)

**Fiscal commitment (over 5 years + ongoing):**
- Recruit: $2B + $262M/yr ongoing
- Train: $331M + $18M/yr ongoing
- Hire: $3.4B + $468M/yr ongoing
- CAF pathway: $250M + $45M/yr ongoing
- Total: ~$5.98B over 5 years, ~$793M/yr ongoing thereafter

**Notable claims worth scrutiny:**
- "Path to certification cut by 50 per cent" — apprenticeships are 3-5 years; halving = 1.5-2.5 years. Engineering-level falsifiable claim. Union officials and apprenticeship boards will have informed views.
- "Reserve Trades Experience Pilot Program" — CAF parallel pipeline; ~1,000-2,000/year incremental trainees est.

**Specific program mechanics (worth citing for piece):**
- SMEs receive $10K wage incentive for first-year apprentice
- Apprentice grant: $400/week during in-class training (up to $16K total)
- Completion bonus: $5K one-time on Red Seal certification
- 5-step pathway: Team Canada Strong Program → register as apprentice → technical training → certify → Red Seal career

**Implication for piece framing:**
The federal text supports Sibley's central argument STRUCTURE. They acknowledge: (a) baseline 1.4M need, (b) ~20K/yr structural shortfall, (c) Build Canada ADDS to demand. They then announce a program sized to (b) — and never quantify or address (c). Sibley computes (c).
