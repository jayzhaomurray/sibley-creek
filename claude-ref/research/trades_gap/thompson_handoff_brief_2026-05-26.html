Thompson handoff brief — Build-Big trades gap

For: Thompson From: Jay Zhao-Murray, Sibley Creek Date: 2026-05-26

The thesis (one sentence)

Carney's recruitment strategy solves the old construction worker shortage while his industrial policy creates another.

The central claim

Canada was already short ~108,000 tradespeople by 2034 before Carney announced Build Canada. The federal Team Canada Strong program — $6B for 80,000-100,000 new Red Seal trades workers over five years — is sized to fill that pre-existing baseline gap. It adds zero headroom for the ~$1 trillion of new pipelines, LNG terminals, SMRs, transmission lines, mines, ports, and doubled housing starts that Build Canada is supposed to deliver. The federal government has published no bridge between the training program and the project portfolio. Sibley builds that bridge. The deliverable is a person-year gap number by province and a trade-priority overlay anchored to BuildForce's published 5-tier NOC classification.

Methodology in three bullets

  • Supply — take BuildForce Canada's CMLF 2025-2034 Highlights as published (national + 10 provincial). They modelled their supply forecast before the federal Major Projects Office existed (BuildForce: April 2025; MPO: August 2025). So their baseline definitionally excludes Build Canada incremental demand.
  • Demand — two methods in order. Method 1: if a project has published workforce numbers (federal IAAC EA, provincial EA, CER, CNSC, proponent IR), use them. Method 2: if not, scale from a comparable completed analog by the matching unit (km for pipelines, MTPA for LNG, MW for nuclear/hydro, dwellings for housing). Flag every analog choice transparently.
  • Validation — phone calls to union locals (UA pipefitters, IBEW, Boilermakers, Operating Engineers, Iron Workers), provincial apprenticeship boards, construction-firm HR (PCL, EllisDon, Aecon, Ledcor), college trades-program faculty, project IR/comms. Named on-the-record where possible. These confirm whether the math matches what people on the ground see.

The scope

The federal Major Projects Office has referred 15 nation-building projects + 6 transformative strategies + the expected Carney-Smith west-coast pipeline (July 1 referral). For demand modelling we count 16 projects: 11 are Method 1, 5 are Method 2. The MPO inventory file ranks them by data confidence and flags the swing factors. The single largest workforce demand if it proceeds is the Carney-Smith pipeline (TMX analog suggests ~67-76K person-years). The Darlington SMR is the highest-confidence Method 1 project. McIlvenna Bay is effectively in commissioning and contributes ~zero to the 2026-2029 peak window — track for completeness, exclude from peak math. Pickering 5-8, Bruce C, and Cedar LNG are NOT MPO-referred (provincial tracks) and stay outside the MPO aggregate; reconcile via BuildForce baseline.
