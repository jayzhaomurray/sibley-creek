# Build-Big: Answered vs Open Map — May 2026

## 1. Executive summary

As of May 2026 the Carney "Build-Big" / "Build Canada" agenda is roughly 13 months into rollout. The **legislative scaffolding is in place** (Bill C-5 Royal Assent June 26, 2025; Free Trade and Labour Mobility in Canada Act in force January 1, 2026), the **fiscal envelope is announced and PBO-assessed** (Budget 2025 + April 28, 2026 Spring Economic Update; $66.9B deficit 2025-26; debt-to-GDP ~41.7% rising slowly), and the **project pipeline is named** (Major Projects Office has referred ~15 projects worth $125B+; second tranche Nov 13, 2025 added ~$56B; Northern tranche March 12, 2026 added four more). The **Canada Strong Fund ($25B over 3 years)** is announced but the mandate, governance, and investment criteria are not yet defined. The **China pivot has been executed** at the diplomatic level (Beijing visit Jan 16, 2026; canola tariff cut 84% → ~15%, EV quota 49,000 at 6.1%) but its durability is unclear.

What is **answered**: the dollar size of every major program, the legislative mechanism, the formal project list, the headline fiscal-multiplier and internal-trade-gain estimates from third parties, and the immediate PBO sustainability assessment. What is largely **open**: actual project IRR / productivity contribution per dollar; whether the MPO has any throughput beyond political designation; how provinces are translating federal liberalization into real flow; whether the Canada Strong Fund is a development bank or a sovereign wealth fund (and what it will buy); the durability of the China pivot post-CUSMA review; and the cumulative composition effect across components.

**Top wedge candidates (preview, expanded in §5):**
1. A **Build-Big Project Tracker** — single dashboard of every MPO-referred project with referral date, decision deadline, capex, status. Nobody runs it.
2. A **productivity-payoff back-of-envelope** — capex-to-GDP-contribution implied by the government's own $1T figure vs. credible empirical multipliers; first-mover honest accounting.
3. An **internal-trade flow tracker** — actual interprovincial-trade-share-of-GDP series before/after CFTA federal exception removal and bilateral MOUs; the empirical test of the IMF's 4-7% claim.
4. A **Canada Strong Fund vs. sovereign-wealth-fund benchmark scorecard** — what would have to be true (mandate, return target, governance, leverage) for the fund to function as either a SWF or a development bank.
5. A **fiscal-room-for-Build-Big** model — interest-burden-to-revenue trajectory (PBO flags rise from 10.6% to 13.2%) under stress scenarios; the binding-constraint case.

## 3. Component-by-component map

### A. Bill C-5 / One Canadian Economy Act

**ANSWERED**
- Statute received Royal Assent June 26, 2025; Free Trade and Labour Mobility in Canada Act came into force January 1, 2026 per Canada Gazette Part II ([SI/2025-107](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-11-19/html/si-tr107-eng.html); [news.gc](https://www.canada.ca/en/intergovernmental-affairs/news/2025/06/legislation-to-build-one-canadian-economy-receives-royal-assent.html)).
- All 53 federal exceptions under CFTA eliminated by June 30, 2025 ([CFIB](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research-economic-analysis/state-of-internal-trade-canadas-progress-on-internal-trade-in-2025); [Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/one-canadian-economy/news/2025/11/government-of-canada-removes-barriers-to-interprovincial-trade-and-labour-mobility.html)).
- Seven provinces (BC, MB, NB, NS, ON, PEI, QC) have adopted comparable legislation; 19 bilateral provincial MOUs signed; pan-Canadian Mutual Recognition Agreement signed November 2025 with goods-recognition implementation target June 2026 ([Canada.ca internal trade page](https://www.canada.ca/en/intergovernmental-affairs/services/internal-trade/federal-investments-internal-trade.html); [BC release](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025JEG0054-001139)).
- Headline GDP gain estimates exist from multiple credible sources: IMF 2025 Article IV (real GDP up to **7%** from full non-geographic barrier removal, ~$210B; services accounts for ~90% of gain) ([IMF Article IV Jan 21, 2026](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/01/21/pr-26012-canada-imf-executive-board-concludes-2025-article-iv-consultation)); MLI / Tombe 2022 update (4.4-7.9% GDP, $110-200B/yr) ([MLI Tombe paper](https://macdonaldlaurier.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/20250220_Internal-trade-barriers-Tombe-PAPER-NEW-FINAL.pdf)); government estimate $200B / $5,100 per capita ([Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/intergovernmental-affairs/services/internal-trade/federal-investments-internal-trade.html)); CFIB endorses ~$200B figure ([CFIB](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research-economic-analysis/state-of-internal-trade-canadas-progress-on-internal-trade-in-2025)).
- IMF further estimates that **halving** internal trade costs could broadly offset the GDP hit of a 10pp US tariff increase — i.e. liberalization is sized in IMF analysis as a tariff-resilience substitute ([IMF release](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/01/21/pr-26012-canada-imf-executive-board-concludes-2025-article-iv-consultation)).

**OPEN**
- *Provincial-barrier residual.* Federal exceptions are gone; the bigger empirical drag is provincial. Supply management (dairy, poultry), trucking, professional engineering, alcohol distribution remain largely intact. **No public number** decomposes how much of the IMF's headline 7% is now actually unlocked vs. still trapped at provincial level ([MLI Herman](https://macdonaldlaurier.ca/internal-trade-barriers-threaten-canada-and-only-federal-action-can-stop-them-lawrence-l-herman-for-inside-policy/)). *Required to answer:* StatCan interprovincial-trade flow series tagged by sector, mapped against the CFTA exception list residual.
- *Real interprovincial-trade flow response.* The hypothesis is testable: did interprovincial trade-as-share-of-GDP move in H2 2025 / Q1 2026 vs. the pre-2025 trend? StatCan publishes Inter-provincial Flows of Goods and Services; **nobody has run the before/after**. *Why nobody has:* the post-treatment window is too short for academic publication; bank desks don't run this series; CFIB tracks barriers but not flows. *Policy-actionable:* yes — if the flow response is small, the CFTA-removal-as-tariff-substitute case is weakened.
- *Mutual-recognition implementation friction.* The June 2026 goods recognition target ([CFIB](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research-economic-analysis/state-of-internal-trade-canadas-progress-on-internal-trade-in-2025)) creates a falsifiable deadline. Will the residual disputes (food and alcohol exclusions, labelling) prevent the headline goal? *No public tracker.*

### B. Major Projects Office / national-interest projects

**ANSWERED**
- MPO launched August 29, 2025; HQ in Calgary; designed as single window with 2-year regulatory review cap ([PM launch](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2025/08/29/prime-minister-carney-launches-new-major-projects-office-fast-track-nation-building-projects)).
- First tranche September 11, 2025 (5 projects, ~$60B+ economic contribution per government): LNG Canada Phase 2; Darlington New Nuclear (SMR); Port of Montréal Contrecœur; McIlvenna Bay Foran Copper Mine; one more ([Canada.ca initial projects](https://www.canada.ca/en/one-canadian-economy/news/2025/09/major-projects-office-of-canada-initial-projects-under-consideration.html)).
- Second tranche November 13, 2025 (~$56B): a major electric transmission line + LNG export facility (BC); three mines (nickel ON, graphite QC, tungsten NB); first hydroelectric dam in Nunavut ([Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/second-tranche-of-projects-referred-to-canada-s-major-projects-office/)).
- Third (Northern) tranche March 12, 2026: Grays Bay Road and Port (~$1.2B); Mackenzie Valley Highway (~$1.5B+); plus two more ([Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-a-list-of-carneys-major-projects-centred-on-the-north/)).
- Indicative project costs from disclosed estimates: LNG Phase 2 ~CA$20B (no firm public estimate); Darlington SMR ~CA$32B (+54% variance band); Contrecœur ~CA$2.1B (+31% variance) ([CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2025/09/11/canadas-national-projects-betting-on-nuclear-lng-while-the-future-waits/)).
- Total referred portfolio: 15 projects, **$125B+** capital, plus 6 transformative strategies ([Canada.ca MPO projects](https://www.canada.ca/en/privy-council/major-projects-office/projects/national.html)).
- Five-factor designation criteria are codified in the Building Canada Act (resilience/autonomy/security; economic benefits; Indigenous interests; clean growth/climate; execution likelihood).

**OPEN**
- *Designation vs. construction-start.* Referral is not designation; designation is not approval; approval is not construction. **No public tracker** maps each project's status against the 2-year clock. Bay Street's first question — "of the 15 referred, how many will actually break ground in 2026? 2027?" — has no published answer. *Why nobody has:* it requires sustained operational tracking; bank desks have one-off project notes but no rolling tracker.
- *Two-year review delivery.* The act's headline promise is 2-year max review; **zero projects have completed a 2-year cycle** because the MPO is only ~9 months old. The first real test will come in late 2027 for the September 2025 referrals.
- *Indigenous consultation hurdle as production constraint.* 14 First Nations are now in active court challenge to Bill C-5 and Ontario Bill 5 ([Indigenous Watchdog](https://www.indigenouswatchdog.org/update/more-legal-battles-likely-as-first-nations-launch-first-lawsuit-against-new-federal-provincial-laws/); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/indigenous/ontario-challenge-bill-c5-9.7074134)). The Mikisew Cree 2018 procedural-duty question is the leverage point. *Open empirical:* will the challenge succeed on a project-by-project basis or as a systemic challenge? Norton Rose Fulbright argues the duty to consult survives the act's exemption provisions but courts have not ruled ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-ca/knowledge/publications/8c43749a/how-the-new-building-canada-act-works)).
- *Capital deployment rate.* The "$125B referred" figure is announced capex over project lifetime, not committed federal spend. **No public reconciliation** of "MPO-tagged announced capex" → "annual deployable construction spend" → "GDP impact in window X." Likely materially smaller than headline.
- *MPO throughput as a process question.* How many staff? Backlog? Cycle-time per project class? **No public operational data.**

### C. Canada Strong Fund (CSF)

**ANSWERED**
- Announced April 27, 2026; $25B over 3 years on a cash basis to seed; arm's-length Crown corp; CEO + qualified independent board; reports through Minister of Finance ([PM release](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/04/27/prime-minister-carney-announces-canada-strong-fund-canadas-first); [DOF](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/04/canada-strong-fund.html)).
- Stated dual mandate: "invest in strategic Canadian projects and companies alongside other investors — with a clear objective to achieve commercial returns to build the wealth of Canada."
- Retail investment product to launch separately.
- Multiple credible critiques concur it is **not** a sovereign wealth fund as the term is conventionally used ([IRPP Policy Options](https://policyoptions.irpp.org/2026/05/canada-strong-fund/); [Globe and Mail opinion](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/opinion/article-canada-strong-sovereign-wealth-fund/); [Canadian Affairs](https://www.canadianaffairs.news/2026/05/01/a-sovereign-wealth-fund-in-name-only/); [Fraser](https://www.fraserinstitute.org/commentary/carney-governments-sovereign-wealth-fund-not-best-way-help-grow-wealth-canada)). Better analogues: BPI France, Canada Infrastructure Bank, KfW. Norway/GIC comparison fails on (a) funding (Norway's is surplus-funded; CSF is borrowed); (b) governance (Norway's separates the fund from political direction); (c) purpose (Norway's invests for return externally; CSF invests for nation-building domestically).
- Initial cash injection is debt-financed; this is the Fraser/Reason critique ([Fraser](https://www.fraserinstitute.org/commentary/carneys-canada-strong-fund-unlikely-make-canada-more-attractive-investors); [Reason](https://reason.com/2026/04/30/canadas-new-sovereign-wealth-fund-is-actually-a-debt-fueled-spending-scheme/)).

**OPEN**
- *Mandate specifics.* Hurdle rate? Risk tolerance? Sector mandate? Geographic mandate? Crowd-in ratio target? The PM release explicitly defers details "in the coming months." **No public specification exists** as of May 25, 2026.
- *Relationship to CIB, BDC, EDC, BCH, Indigenous Loan Guarantee, the Critical Minerals Sovereign Fund.* Five federal investment vehicles now operate in adjacent space; whose mandate is the CSF's that the others don't cover? *Open.*
- *Commercial-return vs. nation-building tension.* If a project clears commercial hurdles, why does it need CSF capital (i.e., what private capital is failing to fund it)? If it doesn't clear, what is the implied subsidy and where does it show up in fiscal accounts? Common-Wealth and academic critics have raised this; **no government response on substance.**
- *Capitalization growth path.* The PM release says the fund will "grow through asset recycling and reinvestment." Concrete: which assets are envisioned to be recycled into it? Trans Mountain? CIB portfolio? **Unanswered.**

### D. Build Communities Strong Fund (BCSF)

**ANSWERED**
- $51B over 10 years starting 2026-27 ($3B/yr ongoing); 3 streams: Provincial/Territorial $17.2B, Direct Delivery $6B, Community $27.8B ([Canada.ca backgrounder](https://www.canada.ca/en/housing-infrastructure-communities/news/2026/04/launch-of-the-build-communities-strong-fund.html); [PM release](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/04/07/prime-minister-carney-launches-build-communities-strong-fund-and)).
- Government claims average 42,000 jobs/yr supported and **$95B GDP contribution over a decade** ([backgrounder](https://www.canada.ca/en/housing-infrastructure-communities/news/2026/04/launch-of-the-build-communities-strong-fund.html)).
- First tranche of projects announced April 7, 2026.

**OPEN**
- *$95B GDP figure derivation.* The $95B-over-10-years projection on $51B of federal spend implies a fiscal multiplier of ~1.86 on cumulative GDP — at the high end of the empirical range (Canadian government estimates ~1.6 short-run; Conference Board 1.11-1.14; cross-country averages ~1.5 with state-dependence) ([Fraser Institute review](https://www.fraserinstitute.org/sites/default/files/fiscal-policy-and-recessions-role-of-public-infrastructure-spending.pdf); [Broadbent / Centre for Spatial Economics](https://www.iuoelocal793.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/Broadbent-Study.pdf)). **No public derivation of the $95B estimate has been released.** Methodology audit is open.
- *Allocation by province / municipality.* The Community Stream is $27.8B over a decade; this maps roughly $750/Canadian/yr but actual flows will be highly variable. **No tracker.**
- *Crowding out vs. additive.* The $51B largely re-baselines previously announced infrastructure programs (Investing in Canada Plan tail; Canada Community-Building Fund). The net **new** federal contribution above baseline has not been published cleanly.

### E. Build Canada Homes (BCH)

**ANSWERED**
- Launched September 14, 2025; $13B initial capitalization; focused on non-market and affordable housing; uses public land + flexible financial tools + modular methods ([PM launch Sep 14, 2025](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2025/09/14/prime-minister-carney-launches-build-canada-homes); [HICC page](https://housing-infrastructure.canada.ca/bch-mc/index-eng.html)).
- 10,000+ units committed since launch; 1,400 under construction or breaking ground imminently ([PM April 23, 2026](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/04/23/prime-minister-carney-delivers-commitment-accelerate-homebuilding)).
- PBO estimate: **26,000 homes over 5 years; 2.1% supply boost; "modest" impact** ([Global News on PBO](https://globalnews.ca/news/11556058/mark-carney-build-canada-homes-pbo/)).
- Campaign pledge to double housing construction to 500,000/yr remains the political target; no quantified bridge from BCH alone to that target.

**OPEN**
- *Bridge from BCH + Build Communities Strong Fund housing components + CMHC + provincial action → 500,000 starts.* Currently ~250K starts/yr; doubling implies +250K incremental starts. The federal contribution at 26,000 over 5 years is a rounding error. **No public public-private decomposition** of what would have to be true elsewhere.
- *Unit cost / per-unit subsidy.* On $13B / 26,000 homes the implicit federal capital-per-unit is ~$500K, which is plausible for affordable construction but **the breakdown by program element has not been published.**
- *Speed.* CMHC Housing Supply Report tracks starts; the empirical question of whether BCH-tagged projects start within 18 months of commitment is testable but **not yet tracked publicly.**

### F. Productivity Super-Deduction

**ANSWERED**
- Budget 2025 announced reinstated Accelerated Investment Incentive + immediate expensing for productivity-enhancing assets (patents, data infrastructure, computers) + immediate expensing for SR&ED capex + new immediate expensing for M&P buildings (phase-out 2030-33) + accelerated depreciation for low-carbon LNG facilities ([McMillan](https://mcmillan.ca/insights/publications/budget-2025-accelerated-depreciation-the-so-called-productivity-super-deduction-combatting-tax-deferral-through-tiered-corporations/); [DOF Budget 2025 chapter 1](https://budget.canada.ca/2025/report-rapport/chap1-en.html)).
- PBO costs the package at **$19.4B over 5 years** beginning 2025-26 ([PBO LEG-2526-012-S](https://www.pbo-dpb.ca/en/publications/LEG-2526-012-S--accelerated-capital-cost-allowance-immediate-expensing-measures--mesures-deduction-amortissement-accelere-passation-charges-immediate)).
- METR drops by >2pp to **13.2%**, claimed lowest in G7 and below OECD average ([DOF](https://budget.canada.ca/2025/report-rapport/chap1-en.html)).
- Designed partly to counter US "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" expensing parity.

**OPEN**
- *Marginal investment elasticity.* The empirical literature on accelerated depreciation effects on actual capex (Zwick & Mahon 2017 for US bonus depreciation; House & Shapiro 2008) is mixed; small-firm response is larger than large-firm. **No Canada-specific elasticity estimate** has been published linking the super-deduction to expected incremental private capex.
- *RBC GAM's incremental 0.5pp growth claim.* RBC has cited an "incremental 0.5% of economic growth" if measures are "executed properly" ([RBC Wealth Management 2026 outlook](https://www.rbcwealthmanagement.com/en-ca/insights/canadas-2026-economic-and-market-outlook-a-double-double-half-full)). The methodology behind that number is not public.
- *Capital deepening transmission.* C.D. Howe established that capital deepening accounted for ~90% of Canadian labour-productivity growth 2000-2019 ([CD Howe Commentary 699](https://cdhowe.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Commentary_699.pdf)). The super-deduction is designed to lift this. **The empirical decomposition of M&E capex per worker before/after super-deduction is not yet visible** (data lag) and is the natural mid-2027 wedge.

### G. Team Canada Strong (skilled trades)

**ANSWERED**
- Announced April 29, 2026 as part of SEU; $6B / 5 years; target 80,000-100,000 new Red Seal workers; $2B for paid placements + $10K first-year-salary support; $3.4B / $468M ongoing for completion ($5K bonus + $400/wk technical-training top-up to $16K total); $331M to modernize training ([PM release](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/04/29/prime-minister-carney-announces-team-canada-strong-nationwide-plan); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/politics/economic-update-budget-skilled-trades-canada-workers-youth-unemployment-9.7180411)).
- BuildForce/government baseline gap: 1.4M additional trades by 2033; ~20,000/yr structural deficit absent intervention.

**OPEN**
- *Provinces own apprenticeship.* Industry voices (DCN op-ed) flag that without provincial buy-in on Red Seal mutual recognition, the program faces friction ([Daily Commercial News](https://canada.constructconnect.com/dcn/news/labour/2026/05/industry-perspectives-op-ed-where-are-the-provinces-in-carneys-team-canada-strong-initiative)). *No public scorecard of provincial participation status.*
- *80,000-100,000 vs. 1.4M.* Even at full delivery the program addresses ~7% of the cumulative 8-year structural need. The bridge from federal program → total trades workforce is **not publicly modelled.**
- *Throughput timing.* Apprenticeships are typically 3-5 years; the program's first cohort completion lands 2029-2031, with the Build-Big project construction wave 2026-2028. **Mismatched timing has not been publicly quantified.**

### H. Critical Minerals

**ANSWERED**
- Critical Minerals Sovereign Fund: $2B over 5 years; equity, debt, and offtake instruments; six mineral focus (copper, nickel, lithium, graphite, cobalt, REE) ([National Observer](https://www.nationalobserver.com/2026/03/04/news/canada-critical-minerals-fund-announcement)).
- First and Last Mile Fund: $1.5B / 2026-2030; mining + infrastructure value-chain enablement ([Canada.ca strategy update](https://www.canada.ca/en/campaign/critical-minerals-in-canada/canadas-critical-minerals-strategy/canadas-critical-minerals-strategy-progress-update.html)).
- March 3, 2026: $165.2M for 22 projects in 8 provinces, unlocking $434M total ([Canada.ca backgrounder](https://www.canada.ca/en/natural-resources-canada/news/2026/03/backgrounder-government-of-canada-invests-to-unlock-canadas-critical-minerals-advantage.html)).
- Commercial milestones: Sayona North American Lithium commercial; Voisey's Bay and Raglan nickel expansions reach completion.

**OPEN**
- *Reserve-to-production timelines.* Critical minerals projects move slowly (10-15 yr typical permit-to-pour). **No public timeline-mapping** of which projects could produce by 2028 vs. 2030 vs. 2035.
- *Offtake leverage.* The fund is empowered for offtake contracts but **no public roster** of US/Korean/Japanese/EU offtake commitments exists in consolidated form.
- *China-pivot tension.* Carney's January China deal exists in the same agenda as the critical-minerals push aimed at reducing dependence on Chinese supply. **The economic-statecraft consistency of these two strands is undertheorized in public commentary.**

### I. Indigenous Loan Guarantee Program (ILGP)

**ANSWERED**
- Doubled from $5B → $10B March 2025 ([Fasken](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2026/04/update-on-trends-in-indigenous-equity-investments-in-canada)).
- Individual guarantees $20M – $1B / transaction.
- First guarantee May 2025: Stonlasec8 ($400M guarantee enabling ~$715M investment in Enbridge Westcoast) ([CILGC](https://cilgc-cgpac.ca/en); [CDEV](https://cdev.gc.ca/second-loan-guarantee-issued-under-the-federal-indigenous-loan-guarantee-program/)).

### Q. Fiscal envelope

**ANSWERED**
- Budget 2025: $280B over 5 yrs federal capital/incentives; mobilizing $1T total (~$315B infrastructure + $270B industrial + $210B R&D + $130B housing + $95B tax incentives + $60B accelerated depreciation).
- SEU 2026:
  - 2025-26 deficit $66.9B (down $11.5B from Budget 2025);
  - 2026-27 deficit $65.3B; gradually declining to $53.2B by 2030-31;
  - Deficits ~2% of GDP and declining modestly.
- PBO ([NT-2627-002-S](https://www.pbo-dpb.ca/en/publications/NT-2627-002-S--pbo-assessment-spring-economic-update-fiscal-anchors-fiscal-sustainability--evaluation-dpb-mise-jour-economique-printemps-cibles-budgetaires-viabilite-financiere)):
  - Debt-to-GDP 1.4pp lower than Budget 2025; rises from 40.7% (2024-25) to ~41.7% avg (2026-27 to 2030-31);
  - Long-run: declines to 25.1% by 2060-61; **"sustainable under the IMF framework with a modest negative fiscal gap"**;
  - **Interest burden rises from 10.6% → 13.2% of revenues by 2030-31 — flagged as concerning.**
- PBO ([NT-2627-003-S](https://www.pbo-dpb.ca/en/publications/NT-2627-003-S--pbo-assessment-spring-economic-update-government-major-capital-priorities--evaluation-dpb-mise-jour-economique-printemps-principales-priorites-investissement-gouvernement-dans)): "As Budget 2025 measures have not yet been fully implemented, it is not yet possible to assess their impact on productivity"; flags housing-supply targets absent; flags labour productivity essentially flat 2022-2025 vs 1.2% historical.

**OPEN**
- *Crowd-in ratio.* The $280B → $1T multiplier implies a 3.6x crowd-in. **No published derivation.** Empirical literature on infrastructure-PPP crowd-in is in the 1.5-2.5x range. The 3.6x figure is at the upper extreme of plausibility.
- *Effective annual incremental capex.* If the federal contribution is $280B / 5yr = $56B/yr, and only a fraction is new above baseline (likely 40-60%), then the net new federal contribution is ~$25-35B/yr. **The above-baseline math is not in the PBO assessment.**
- *Interest-burden stress scenarios.* PBO flags 13.2% by 2030-31 as concerning. **No publicly modelled BoC-rate-shock case** showing what 50-100bp higher rates do to that path. With $66.9B current deficits and ~$1.4T federal debt, +100bp adds ~$14B/yr in steady state.
- *Provincial fiscal interaction.* The federal Build-Big requires provincial co-funding (transit, hospitals). Provincial debt paths are far weaker (Ontario, Quebec) than federal. **No consolidated general-government fiscal-sustainability picture under the Build-Big assumptions.**

## 4. Cross-cutting open questions

1. **What is the realized productivity contribution per dollar of Build-Big capex deployed?** Bottom-up by component: super-deduction, MPO portfolio, BCSF, BCH. *Required:* StatCan capital stock + productivity accounts re-cut against federal-program-coded capex. *Why open:* nobody has both the granular project-coding and the discipline to assemble it. *Action:* high (BoC, DoF, PBO would all read).

2. **Is the $1T mobilization claim consistent with the empirical literature on public-investment crowd-in?** $280B federal → $1T total ⇒ 3.6x. World Bank / IMF literature pegs infrastructure-PPP at 1.5-2.5x. The Build-Big methodology paper has not been released. *Falsifiable:* yes, with a sector-by-sector bottom-up reconstruction.

3. **Which Build-Big components produce measurable outputs in the 2026-2028 window vs. require 5+ year horizons?** Quick wins: Productivity Super-Deduction (effect in 12-24 months); CFTA internal-trade flow response (testable now); BCH housing starts. Slow burn: MPO project completions (2028+); CSF returns (5+ yrs); critical-minerals production (5-15 yrs); pipeline (2030+); 3.5% defence (2035). *Required:* component-by-component timeline mapping. *Open: nobody has assembled it.*

4. **Does the SWF + fast-tracked projects + internal-trade liberalization + super-deduction compose into productivity uplift greater than the sum of parts, or do they crowd each other (and the private sector)?** *Falsifiable* via interaction-term analysis once enough post-treatment data exists. Currently theoretical.

5. **What's the implicit fiscal-multiplier assumption in the government's projections, and how does it compare to empirical multipliers for similar program types internationally?** BCSF $51B → $95B GDP over a decade implies cumulative multiplier of 1.86 — at the upper end of empirical multipliers. *Audit-able from public documents.*

6. **What's the binding constraint on MPO throughput — staffing? Indigenous consultation? Provincial cooperation? Proponent due diligence? Capital markets?** A diagnostic of *why* a project moves quickly vs. slowly within the same 2-year cap. *Required:* operational data from MPO + project-by-project tracking. Currently invisible.

7. **At what BoC policy rate / world risk-free-rate does the Build-Big fiscal envelope become unsustainable?** PBO declares it sustainable with a modest negative fiscal gap under current rates; **stress scenarios are not public.** Falsifiable via reverse-engineered debt-dynamics.

8. **What is the empirically realized interprovincial trade flow response to federal CFTA exception removal (Jan 1, 2026 in force)?** The first six-month flow data lands ~September 2026. **First out wins this number.**

9. **Does the China pivot represent a net trade-diversification gain or a notional gain that is overwhelmed by retaliatory US tariff escalation?** Net real-export change to China vs. lost net real-export change to US, post Jan 16 2026. *Empirical, lagged ~8 weeks.*

10. **What is the demographic-policy / Build-Big-labour-demand consistency?** Trades program targets 80-100K new workers over 5 yrs while temp residents cut 287K/yr and PR cut 15K/yr (i.e., the net labour-input reduction is an order of magnitude larger than what the Trades program adds). *No public reconciliation.*

## 5. Top wedge candidates

The screening criteria (topicality + 4-8 week producibility + ownable artifact + advances conversation + public-data accessibility) point to five.

### Wedge 1 — The Build-Big Project Tracker (recurring artifact)

**The artifact.** A single dashboard listing all MPO-referred projects (15+), columns: tranche date, referral date, designated yes/no, designation date, decision deadline (2-yr clock), proponent, federal contribution (where disclosed), provincial co-funding, Indigenous-equity participation status, court-challenge status, construction start (forecast vs. actual), capex by stage. Updated monthly.

**Why it qualifies.**
- *Topicality:* The single most-cited Build-Big artifact ("17 projects, $126B" / "15 projects, $125B+") is a federal slide and not a tracker — every Bay Street, BoC, Finance, PBO desk would read this.
- *Producibility:* Source documents are public (Canada.ca/MPO; PM releases; provincial regulatory dockets; Indigenous Watchdog for court status). A 3-4 week scaffold + 1-week production loop.
- *Ownability:* First-mover; the URL becomes the citation.
- *Advancement:* Replaces narrative ("Carney is moving fast / slow") with measurable cycle-time.
- *Accessibility:* Zero proprietary data needed.

### Wedge 2 — Productivity-payoff back-of-envelope (single chart)

**The artifact.** One chart + 500-word explainer: federal Build-Big capex implied annual flow vs. (a) empirical fiscal-multiplier range from peer literature, (b) productivity-elasticity-to-capital from C.D. Howe / StatCan, (c) the government's own implied numbers ($95B from BCSF, $1T mobilization, $60B from infrastructure). Result: a defensible Sibley range for the productivity uplift the program could plausibly produce, vs. what the government claims, vs. what the PBO is willing to certify.

**Why it qualifies.**
- *Topicality:* This is the single biggest argument in Canadian macro right now.
- *Producibility:* Pure desk work; 2-3 weeks.
- *Ownability:* The chart becomes the citation in every subsequent Build-Big debate.
- *Advancement:* Replaces government PR numbers with a methodology-anchored range. Honest accounting on the implicit crowd-in.
- *Accessibility:* All inputs public.

### Wedge 3 — Internal-trade flow tracker (recurring artifact)

**The artifact.** StatCan Inter-provincial Trade Flows series re-cut as a share of GDP, monthly, broken by goods vs. services, vs. the IMF-implied counterfactual. Pre-CFTA-exception removal baseline (2017-2024) vs. post (H2 2025 / Q1 2026 vs. forward). The empirical test of the 7%-GDP-gain hypothesis.

**Why it qualifies.**
- *Topicality:* Internal-trade lane is one of the most-debated components; everybody quotes the IMF/MLI numbers; nobody has the flow data tracking the response.
- *Producibility:* 3-4 weeks; StatCan series exist (Table 12-10-0088 / Inter-provincial Trade Flows).
- *Ownability:* First-mover; the only place this is tracked publicly.
- *Advancement:* Closes the loop between aspirational forecasts and realized response.
- *Accessibility:* Public StatCan data; some manual seasonal-adjustment work.

### Wedge 4 — Canada Strong Fund mandate scorecard (single deliverable)

**The artifact.** A 1,500-word deep dive + scorecard table: what would have to be true (governance, hurdle rate, sector mandate, leverage ratio, board structure, public-disclosure regime) for the CSF to function as (a) a true sovereign wealth fund in the Norway/GIC sense, (b) a development bank in the BPIFrance/KfW sense, (c) a strategic-equity instrument in the Mubadala/Temasek sense. Score the announced design against each archetype, identify the open design choices, and lay out a falsifiable rubric for evaluating the eventual mandate document when it lands.

### Wedge 5 — Build-Big fiscal-room model (decision-relevant tool)

**The artifact.** A simple, open, reproducible model: debt-to-GDP path + interest-burden-to-revenue ratio under (a) PBO baseline, (b) BoC +50bp scenario, (c) BoC +100bp scenario, (d) recession scenario, (e) US-CUSMA-failure scenario. Computes the BoC policy rate at which the PBO's "modest negative fiscal gap" disappears. Pairs with PBO's NT-2627-002-S as the public stress-test companion.

**Implementation rank by quickest-to-defensible-artifact.** Wedge 2 (1-2 weeks) < Wedge 4 (2-3 weeks) < Wedge 3 (3-4 weeks) < Wedge 5 (3-4 weeks) < Wedge 1 (4 weeks initial + monthly recurring).

**Highest reach-by-citation.** Wedge 1 > Wedge 3 > Wedge 2 > Wedge 5 > Wedge 4. (Trackers and flow series get cited more than scorecards.)
