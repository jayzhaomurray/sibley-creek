# Canadian macro coverage landscape — May 2026

**Purpose.** Map who covers what in Canadian macro, free and paid, so Sibley
Creek can identify wide-open lanes for a "big first story."

**Method.** Web research May 2026; bylines and rosters re-verified against 2026
output. Pricing tiers from public marketing pages. Stale 2023-2024 rosters
discarded — Conference Board rebranded to Signal49 in Jan 2026; Frances Donald
moved Manulife → RBC; Yves Giroux is "former PBO" by 2026; Andrew Bell and
Amber Kanwar left BNN Bloomberg; Royce Mendes is at Desjardins (left CIBC 2022).
Lapointe at Manulife is **Dominique**, not Maxime.

**Conventions.** Cadence = how often the named publisher emits material on that
topic. "Tier" = audience: M = mass / retail (free dailies, blogs), I = industry
(banks, finance pros, sell-side), II = institutional (asset managers, central
banks, sovereigns), P = policy / academic.

## 2. Topic saturation ranking

| Topic | Saturation | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| Headline CPI / inflation prints | **SATURATED** | Six bank desks + StatCan + BoC + every reporter + Globe/Bloomberg/Reuters all write on print-day. Marginal reader gets nothing new from a 7th source. |
| BoC policy decisions | **SATURATED** | BoC + 6 bank desks + C.D. Howe MPC + Globe/Bloomberg/Reuters + Hub + Policy Options. The most-covered story in Canadian macro. |
| LFS (jobs report) | **SATURATED** | Bank desks publish within 30 min of 8:30 ET print; wire stories + Globe within the hour. |
| CREA housing prints | **SATURATED** | CREA + Tal + bank desks + Better Dwelling + CMT News + Globe/FP. Tal alone owns the framing. |
| Big bank earnings macro reads | **SATURATED** | Bank-equity analysts + bank econ desks cross-publish. |
| Federal budget / Spring update | **SATURATED** | DoF + PBO + IFSD + C.D. Howe + Hub + Globe + every bank desk. |
| Tariffs / USMCA framing | **MODERATE → SATURATED** | RBC tariff tracker; TD industry-pressure-points page; Oxford Economics deep dive; Globe/Reuters/Bloomberg daily. The trade beat has been the busiest of 2025-26. |
| Productivity / capital deepening | **MODERATE** | C.D. Howe (Robson, Cross); McKinsey one-off; OECD; Policy Options; The Hub; bank desks episodic. Heavily framed by think tanks, **rarely tracked with live data**. |
| Provincial economies | **MODERATE** | TD Provincial Forecast is the franchise; Signal49 has the historic dataset; provincial stats agencies + budgets. Coverage is uneven province to province. |
| Household debt / DTI / DSR | **MODERATE** | StatCan releases drive bank-desk write-ups quarterly; Tal speaks publicly; Better Dwelling on retail side. |
| Immigration & labour market integration | **MODERATE** | C.D. Howe + IRCC + RBC "Top Risks" + Moffatt. Mostly policy commentary; less primary data extraction. |
| OSFI/banking macroprudential | **MODERATE** | OSFI publishes raw; C.D. Howe + bank-equity analysts comment; very little independent econ-commentary. |
| BoC research / staff papers | **THIN** | BoC publishes 30+ SAN/SAP/SDP per year; **almost no journalistic translation**. The MPR gets covered, the papers behind it don't. |
| Senior Loan Officer Survey | **THIN** | BoC releases quarterly; one or two desks mention it; **almost zero deep coverage**. |
| Provincial fiscal sustainability (debt path, structural deficits) | **THIN** | PBO Fiscal Sustainability Report (annual); Tombe paper; otherwise sporadic. **No monthly tracker**. |
| Regional CPI / sub-component sticky-price decomposition | **THIN** | StatCan provides the data; bank desks reference; **no live dashboard, no monthly persistence note**. |
| Capital flows / FDI / TIC-equivalent / loonie reserve share | **THIN** | StatCan BOP quarterly; **one TD note** (Lapointe) on financial flows; otherwise periodic. The Yahoo Finance "loonie dumped from reserves" story was a one-off. |
| Canadian FCI (financial conditions index) | **ABSENT** | BoC has retired MCI; **no live, named, public Canadian FCI** that gets cited weekly. | Port traffic / high-frequency real-economy | **THIN** | Marine-traffic APIs exist; **no econ-publication consolidates them** as a Canada-export pulse. |
| Nowcasting Canadian GDP (weekly) | **THIN** | BDLNow (Canadian Chamber, weekly) + IFSD @NowcastCanada are the two; bank desks don't run weekly nowcasts publicly. |
| Wages by sector (granular SEPH cuts) | **THIN** | StatCan provides 14,960+ series; **almost nobody dissects them publicly** beyond aggregate framing. BoC's wage-microdata SAN (2024-23) is the rare exception. |
| Mortgage renewal wave / payment shock cohorts | **THIN-MODERATE** | CMHC RMIR is twice yearly; Tal speaks; **nobody publishes a monthly cohort tracker**. |
| Independent / non-bank-aligned Canadian macro Substack | **THIN** | Deer Point Macro is the only one with traction; otherwise the gap is the whole point. |

## 3. Wide-open lane callouts

These are the specific places where Sibley Creek could publish original work that
nobody else does — or where the only people doing it are paid services charging
institutional fees.

### 3.1 A live, named Canadian Financial Conditions Index
**Why it's open.** No public Canadian FCI runs weekly. The BoC retired its MCI;
the 2004 BoC working paper FCIs are academic; the Chicago Fed NFCI has no
Canadian analogue. Bank desks reference "financial conditions" anecdotally. A
named, reproducible, weekly-updated Sibley-Creek FCI (rates + spreads + FX +
equity + housing risk premium) is the kind of artifact that gets cited.

### 3.2 Provincial fiscal sustainability — monthly cadence
**Why it's open.** PBO does the long-run Fiscal Sustainability Report once a
year. Tombe writes on it episodically. Provincial Fiscal Monitors are buried in
each province's budget secretariat website. **There is no monthly cross-province
dashboard** decomposing structural vs cyclical deficit, debt-to-GDP path, and
debt-charges-to-revenue ratio. This is a 13-jurisdiction matrix everyone needs
and nobody publishes.

### 3.3 Mortgage renewal wave — monthly cohort tracker
**Why it's open.** Everyone agrees this matters; CMHC RMIR is twice-yearly; Tal
talks about it; OSFI has the raw data. **Nobody publishes a monthly cohort
table** showing: of all mortgages originated in 2020-22 at sub-2% rates, what
share has renewed, what payment shock cohort-by-cohort, what's the arrears
response so far. Build it from CBA + OSFI M4 + CMHC, publish monthly. Banking
analysts at TD/RBC/BMO would read it.

### 3.4 BoC research-paper translation channel
**Why it's open.** BoC publishes 30+ Staff Analytical Notes, Staff Analytical
Papers, Staff Discussion Papers, and Staff Working Papers per year. The MPR gets
covered; almost none of the underlying papers do. A "what the BoC just
published, in 400 words" weekly column with the chart would be uniquely
positioned given Jay's ex-Bloomberg econ-editor background. Closest analogue is
NBER's daily digest — nothing equivalent for BoC.

### 3.5 Senior Loan Officer Survey deep read — quarterly
**Why it's open.** BoC publishes the SLOS; one or two bank desks reference it in
passing; the survey itself is hard to read. A standing quarterly Sibley analysis
of SLOS + parallel US Fed SLOOS comparison, with the credit-availability index
trajectory, would have no competitor.

### 3.6 Regional sticky-price / persistence decomposition (monthly with CPI)
**Why it's open.** StatCan publishes CPI by province and by 200+ basket items.
BoC publishes CPI-trim/median/common nationally. **Nobody publishes a monthly
persistence decomposition** showing which sub-baskets are driving the divergence
between provinces, and which prices are sticky vs flexible in the Bils-Klenow
sense. This is the kind of thing a serious central-bank watcher wants.

### 3.7 Capital flows / FDI / portfolio flow Canadian dashboard
**Why it's open.** StatCan BOP is quarterly and dense. The Yahoo headline that
the Canadian dollar is being dumped from reserves at record pace is a one-off.
TD's Lapointe wrote one good note. **Nobody runs a quarterly capital-flows
dashboard for Canada** — FDI inflows by source country, portfolio debt vs
equity, official reserves share, IIP composition. This is bread-and-butter at
the IMF; nobody publishes it in Canada in plain English.

### 3.8 Sub-sectoral wage tracker (SEPH detail beyond the aggregate)
**Why it's open.** SEPH has 14,000+ series. Coverage stops at "wages up X%
overall." A monthly Sibley table on the 20 most economically-significant
sub-industries (healthcare, construction, finance, retail, public-sector, etc.)
with hourly earnings + hours-worked dynamics would beat what any current
publication does. Especially valuable for productivity discussion.

### 3.9 High-frequency / nowcasting weekly tracker
**Why it's open.** Two trackers exist publicly (BDLNow, IFSD @NowcastCanada);
neither is heavily promoted; neither does post-mortems comparing nowcast vs
realized GDP. A Sibley weekly "where's the economy this week" — with 10-15
high-frequency series in a single chart pack + the BDL/IFSD comparison + a
nowcast disagreement read — is a recurring product nobody publishes.

### 3.10 Port traffic / high-frequency export pulse
**Why it's open.** Vancouver, Montreal, Halifax port data exists at marine-AIS
APIs and at port-authority operations centres. Nobody on the econ side
consolidates this into "how Canada's export sector looks this week." Especially
relevant in the tariff cycle.

### 3.11 Mode-3 commentary on what the bank desks are saying
**Why it's open.** A weekly "Big-Six in dispersion" piece — where are the bank
forecasts converging, where are they fragmenting, what's the spread between
hawkish (NBC, RBC) and dovish (Desjardins) projections — is a Mode 3 use of bank
content that doesn't compete with them. Nobody publishes a structured weekly
read of Big-Six forecaster disagreement.

### 3.12 What the C.D. Howe MPC says vs what the BoC does — running scorecard
**Why it's open.** MPC is a public shadow board with 6 bank chiefs + 6
academics; voting record is public for each meeting. **Nobody runs a "MPC
calls vs BoC decisions" historical scorecard** showing where the shadow board
was right or wrong relative to what BoC ended up doing.

### 3.13 IIP / NIIP / external balance sheet of Canada
**Why it's open.** StatCan publishes IIP quarterly. **No public dashboard**
decomposes Canada's external balance sheet — assets vs liabilities by category,
net international investment position, currency mismatch. Particularly relevant
given the loonie's loss of reserve-currency share in 2025.

### 3.14 Provincial wage / cost-of-living differential tracker
**Why it's open.** Implicit in CPI + SEPH but nobody publishes a clean
provincial real-wage table. With newcomer integration patterns evolving and
internal migration shifting, this would be valuable.

### 3.15 BoC speech sentiment / hawkishness tracker
**Why it's open.** BoC Governing Council members give 20-30 speeches/year.
Nobody runs a structured tracker of language drift across speeches (a "hawkmeter"
of Macklem/Rogers/Kozicki/Mendes/Gravelle vs the MPR baseline). This is one of
those things US Fed-watchers do with FOMC dots; Canadian equivalent doesn't
exist.

## 4. What's commoditized (do not compete here)

Sibley Creek should treat the following as low-marginal-value publishing — the
print-day commentary lane where the marginal reader gets nothing new:

- **CPI print-day commentary** (six bank desks publish within 30 min; Globe and
  wires cover same hour)
- **LFS print-day commentary** (same)
- **BoC rate-decision day reaction** (every desk + every wire + every commentator)
- **CREA monthly housing print** (CREA itself + Tal + bank desks + Better Dwelling
  + CMT News + Globe/FP)
- **Federal budget / Spring update reaction** (PBO + IFSD + C.D. Howe + every
  desk + Hub + Globe + every reporter — Sibley as the 14th voice adds nothing
  unless it has a unique angle)
- **Big bank quarterly earnings macro reads** (covered by equity analysts and
  bank econ desks together)
- **Whatever the latest US tariff announcement is** (RBC tariff tracker + TD +
  Oxford + every wire — Sibley should focus on second-order effects, not the
  announcement itself)
- **GDP advance estimate commentary** (every desk)

**The general rule.** If StatCan or BoC just dropped a print and the bank desks
will all publish within the hour, Sibley should not be in that race. Sibley's
edge is in the **second-order extraction** — what the print implies about a
structural question that takes more than an hour to think about — and in the
**recurring artifact products** (FCI, fiscal-sustainability tracker, mortgage
cohort tracker) that nobody else maintains.

## 5. Quick takeaways for "big first story" selection

The strongest "big first story" candidates from this map are the ones where
(a) primary data exists publicly, (b) nobody currently consolidates it
publicly, and (c) the topic is high-stakes enough that Bay Street and Ottawa
would notice:

1. **The mortgage renewal wave cohort tracker** — directly relevant to bank
   stability, household stress, BoC policy. Tal owns the talking points but
   nobody owns the data artifact.
2. **The Canadian FCI** — high-status artifact; would get picked up by every
   bank desk and BoC-watcher; Chicago Fed NFCI analogue.
3. **Provincial fiscal sustainability dashboard** — Tombe-adjacent academic
   territory but nobody runs it as a recurring product; PMO/Finance/PBO would
   all be readers.
4. **Capital flows / loonie reserve-share story** — the Yahoo headline of late
   2025 had legs but no follow-up; original work decomposing IIP and reserve
   composition would be uniquely Sibley.
5. **BoC research-paper translation channel** — lowest-cost first product;
   sustainable cadence; ex-Bloomberg credential matters here.

Items 1, 2, and 3 are the most defensible as a "big first story" because they
produce a named artifact that the rest of the publication chain has to cite.
