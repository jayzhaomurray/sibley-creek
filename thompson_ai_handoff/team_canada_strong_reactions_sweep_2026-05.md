# Team Canada Strong reactions sweep — 4 weeks post-announcement

**Window:** April 29, 2026 (announcement) through May 25, 2026 (today)
**Purpose:** Determine whether anyone has publicly made the comparison Sibley is contemplating — Team Canada Strong (80-100K Red Seal trades by 2030-31) sized against BuildForce's 108,300 residual construction shortfall to 2034 AND against the incremental labour demand from the Build Canada / MPO project portfolio (~$125B+ in 15-21 referred projects, plus housing/infrastructure/private investment).
**Author:** macro-research-department researcher
**Date:** 2026-05-25

---

## 1. Headline finding

**No one has made the specific Sibley comparison publicly.** Across 4 weeks of bank desk notes, think-tank commentary, mainstream business press, trade press, industry associations, unions, and opposition voices, nobody has explicitly sized the Team Canada Strong target (80-100K by 2030-31) against the residual gap BuildForce has already booked (108,300 to 2034) AND the incremental labour demand from Carney's Build Canada / MPO portfolio.

The closest anyone has come — in rough order of analytical proximity to the Sibley wedge:

1. **Paul de Jong, CEO of Progressive Contractors Association of Canada (PCA)**, in a stakeholder reaction printed by Daily Commercial News on May 4 (Don Wall), framed the program as needing alignment with the Major Projects Office workload: *"With Canada's Major Projects Office underway to facilitate growth in infrastructure and capital projects, a commensurate focus must be on ensuring the skilled trades workforce is of the size, skill and productive capability to meet the challenge."* This is the closest verbal articulation of the alignment question, but de Jong does not quantify either side or arbitrate sufficiency. It is a "we need to make sure these match" statement, not a "we have calculated they do not match" statement.

2. **Marc Desormeaux (VP Policy, Business Council of Canada)**, quoted by Mark Rendell in The Globe and Mail on April 28: *"Think of it as a human-resources strategy to go alongside the major projects capital investment strategy."* This is the framing-level twin of the Sibley question — Desormeaux explicitly couples the two strategies — but he treats them as paired, not as compared. No sizing critique.

3. **Canadian Chamber of Commerce ("Policy Matters" blog, May 12, unsigned)** put the program target on one side of an arithmetic gap with the broader trades need: *"Canada's well-known labour shortages mean that we need 380,000 new bodies in construction by 2034 to build everything we want and need to."* This is the only piece in the sweep that places the program target (80-100K by 2030-31) in the same paragraph as a concrete demand figure (380,000 by 2034) and implies a shortfall. It does not cite BuildForce by name, does not break out the Build Canada portfolio's incremental ask, and does not work through the arithmetic. It is the closest *quantitative* gesture.

4. **Deloitte Canada's "Builders, baby, builders? The half a million worker question"** (Trevin Stratton, Alicia Macdonald, Theo Argitis) is the most analytically substantive Canadian piece on the labour-demand side — it sizes housing, public infrastructure, and private investment demand at 410,000-520,000 additional workers by 2030 and gross recruiting need above 800,000 once retirements are factored in. **It does not engage Team Canada Strong directly** (the piece predates the announcement and does not appear to have been updated; the Canadian Press has cited its numbers in reactive coverage). Deloitte sized the demand; nobody has yet placed the federal program on the same axis as the Deloitte demand stack.

5. **Mortgage Professionals Canada (Liezel Once, April 28)** flagged the sufficiency question in operational terms: *"if the plan falls short, Canada will remain stuck with a housing strategy that promises more homes than its workforce can realistically build."* No BuildForce or MPO comparison.

6. **PBO (Annette Ryan, May 4)** noted that Team Canada Strong is "positioned to mitigate labour supply constraints" and called it "a key success factor" — but the PBO stopped short of arbitrating sufficiency. No sizing analysis.

**Meta-confirmation: the Sibley wedge is clean.** The trades-shortage commentary published since April 29 is overwhelmingly stakeholder-celebratory (unions, industry, training bodies); the more analytical voices (Desormeaux, Chamber, Mortgage Professionals, PBO) gesture at the alignment question without doing the arithmetic; the only piece that did the demand-side arithmetic (Deloitte) predates the announcement; and nobody has connected the 108,300 BuildForce residual specifically to the program target. The combination Sibley is contemplating — BuildForce baseline + Build Canada/MPO incremental + Team Canada Strong target, in one arithmetic — has not been published anywhere I can find in the 4-week window.
