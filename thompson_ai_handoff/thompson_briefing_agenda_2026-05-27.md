## 1. The thesis (5 min)

Goal: Thompson internalizes the framing in one breath.

Talking points:
- The one-line pitch, verbatim: **"Carney's recruitment strategy solves the old construction worker shortage while his industrial policy creates another."** Wojtek validated this language yesterday — that's the line the piece argues.
- The logical chain in three steps: (1) BuildForce says Canada is short ~108K tradespeople by 2034 — that's the baseline; (2) Team Canada Strong, $6B for 80-100K new Red Seal by 2030-31, is sized exactly to that baseline gap; (3) BuildForce's April 2025 forecast predates the Major Projects Office (August 2025), so the MPO project portfolio is definitionally incremental demand that the federal training program does not address.
- The wedge: the federal government acknowledges in the Spring Economic Update that "the scale of investments in housing and infrastructure projects will add to this demand" — and then announces a program that doesn't quantify or address it. We compute what they didn't.
- What we are NOT claiming: Build Canada is impossible, a precise point estimate, or confident timeline-slippage predictions. We're claiming the federal labour math doesn't close, with a range and named uncertainty.

## 3. The methodology (10 min)

Goal: Thompson can defend each methodology choice on a phone call.

Talking points:
- It's arithmetic. Supply minus demand equals gap. No econometrics.
- Supply: take BuildForce's CMLF 2025-2034 Highlights as-is. National + 10 provincial reports, all free, all public. We do not replicate their model — we use their published numbers. Their 5-tier provincial NOC classification anchors the trade-priority overlay.
- Demand, Method 1: published workforce numbers from federal IAAC EA, provincial EA, CER (pipelines), CNSC (nuclear), proponent IR/investor disclosure. EA Project Description sections almost always include peak workforce and total person-years; some include trade-mix breakdowns.
- Demand, Method 2: analog-plus-scaling when no published numbers exist. Reference projects: TMX for oil pipelines (~58 person-years/km), Coastal GasLink for gas pipelines, LNG Canada Phase 1 for LNG (~535 peak/MTPA), Site C for hydro (~7 person-years/MW main civils), Vogtle for conventional nuclear (with caveat: SMR analogs are limited), Inuvik-Tuktoyaktuk for northern roads. Scale linearly by the matching unit; flag every analog choice in the methodology box.
- Validation: phone calls. Union locals, apprenticeship boards, construction-firm HR, college faculty, project IR. Functionally substitutes for BuildForce's confidential regional LMI committees but with named on-the-record sources. Unions WANT this story told — friendly territory.
- The incremental-vs-displacement question: is Team Canada Strong's 80-100K target incremental to BuildForce's 272K already-counted youth recruitment, or the same people the federal program just helps fund? Both readings strengthen the gap argument but require different framing. The piece must answer this explicitly. Watch for it as you work.

Walk Thompson through the inventory file on screen. Talking points:

- The dominant swing factor is the **Carney-Smith west-coast pipeline.** No proponent committed; expected MPO referral July 1; TMX is the canonical analog (67,423 person-years over 1,150 km). Scaling gives 67-76K person-years — that's the same order of magnitude as the entire BuildForce baseline shortfall. If it proceeds, it alone moves the headline.
- Highest-confidence Method 1: Darlington SMR (OPG names specific trades: iron workers, millwrights, boilermakers — best trade-mix disclosure in the inventory), Contrecoeur Terminal (already broken ground, EA #80116 approved), Red Chris Expansion, Canada Nickel Crawford, Matawinie Graphite.

  - **Pickering 5-8, Bruce C, and Cedar LNG are NOT on the MPO list.** They're Ontario / BC provincial-track projects. They're in BuildForce's baseline already. Citation-flag them, exclude from MPO aggregate, reconcile via BuildForce baseline.
