## Open questions for editorial-director

1. **GDP element 1 + Inflation element 1 -- "surprise vs consensus".** No primary public source. Reuters / Bloomberg consensus polls are aggregations of Big Six and other sell-side calls; using them as "consensus" in basics-layer prose is in tension with Sec 7 voice principle ("The Big-Six economics desks are competitors, not sources... We do not cite them in running prose"). Three options: (a) drop the surprise framing entirely from v1 basics; (b) cite "Reuters poll consensus" as the aggregator on the grounds that the aggregation is itself published; (c) use BoC's own MPR-published projection as the anchor for surprise (this is methodologically clean for GDP and CPI -- BoC's central projection is a public number and divergence from BoC's own track is a defensible "surprise"). **Recommendation: option (c) for v1 basics.** Surprise framed as "0.3pp above the BoC's April MPR central projection" carries genuine editorial weight, anchors to a Canadian primary source, and avoids Big Six citation.

2. **GDP element 3 -- business vs residential investment split.** EDR specifies separation. boc-tracker has only the aggregate GFCF contribution. Build it for v1 (small pipeline addition) or simplify the contributions chart to six bars (consumption / government / GFCF / inventories / exports / imports)? **Recommendation: simplify to six bars for v1 basics; the business-vs-residential split is genuinely deep-dive territory (Pillar D productivity gap depends on the cyclical capex inflection).**

3. **GDP element 6 -- C.D. Howe Business Cycle Council recession state.** Not a numerical series; manually maintained. **Recommendation: a one-row research-index entry stating the current BCC verdict, last-update date, and a link to the latest communiqué. Refreshed on each new BCC publication.** Show this on the GDP basics page as a labeled status row, not a chart.

4. **Inflation element 2 -- core trio composition.** EDR names trim and median only. BoC's preferred trio (trim, median, common) is its January 2017 standard; common has been deprioritized in MPR communication since late 2022. **Recommendation: surface trim + median as primary; show common in a hover / footnote with a one-line "BoC has deprioritized common as a core indicator since late 2022; included here for historical comparability".**

5. **Inflation element 3 -- four-state breadth typology.** EDR re-introduces it; May 2026 verification audit retired it as a forced classification (Tier 3 Q3 decision). The four state labels ("broad-based pressure / softening / clustered / polarized") are useful prose vocabulary; the four-state taxonomy as a *classification* was the audit defect. **Recommendation: surface the share-above-3% / share-between / share-below-1% as the breadth element; reserve the four state names as available prose vocabulary when the breadth signal genuinely matches one; do not present the four states as an exhaustive partition.** This both honors the EDR's framing intent and the audit's correction.

6. **Inflation element 6 -- pass-through.** Is the basics-layer ambition a single panel (two side-by-side scatters or strip charts) or two? Construction depends on goods-ex-energy and services-ex-shelter being available. **If those derivations slip, recommend deferring pass-through to v1.5 and showing only the five non-pass-through elements in basics.** Pass-through is the most analytically loaded basics element; it sits closest to deep-dive territory.

7. **Inflation element 4 -- "where the residual stickiness sits".** This is interpretation, not data, and is built monthly by the analyst. Confirm editorial expects this as a one-sentence call-out in the basics blurb (not a separate chart).
