01 · Headline CPI

Headline CPI rose to 2.8%[6] in April on higher gasoline prices.

Headline CPI, m/m and Y/Y · Apr 2026
Headline inflation climbed to 2.8% year-over-year in April[6] from 2.4% in March[7], lifted by higher global energy prices tied to the war in Iran[8]. The print came in below the 3.1% consensus[9], and the monthly move was modest at 0.4% unadjusted[10] and 0.3% seasonally adjusted[11]. Headline remains inside the 1-to-3% control band[12], and the source of the lift is an energy shock the Bank of Canada can look through.

02 · Preferred cores

Core measures ticked down in April, closer to the 2% target[17].

Core-trim and core-median, Y/Y · Apr 2026
Core-trim fell to 2.0% in April[13] and core-median to 2.1%[14], each two-tenths lower than March[15] and lower again than three months ago[16]. Both measures now sit within a tenth of the 2%[13] target — effectively where the Bank of Canada wants them. The energy lift in headline has not bled into the underlying signal — the only read that matters for the rate path.

04 · Sub-aggregates

Gas and energy drove April's lift while the rest of the basket stayed soft.

Shelter, services, goods, food, energy, Y/Y · Apr 2026
Energy ran at 19.2%[22] year-over-year in April on higher global oil and gasoline prices[23], the source of nearly all of the headline acceleration. The rest of the basket is well-behaved: shelter at 1.8%[24] is a fourth straight month below the 2% target[26][25], services eased to 1.7%[27], food slowed to 3.5%[28], and goods at 4.4%[29] looks hot only because energy sits inside it — strip those components and goods inflation is mild. That mix is why the Bank of Canada can absorb an energy-driven headline without changing course.

05 · Expectations

Expectations kept moderating as the headline jumped.

Consumer and firm inflation expectations · Q1 2026 (CSCE / BOS)
Five-year consumer inflation expectations in the CSCE eased to 3.0% in Q1[30], down from a recent-cycle peak of 3.7% in mid-2025[31]. On the firm side, the share of businesses expecting inflation above 3%[32] has collapsed to 11%[33], from around 40%[34] as recently as early 2024[35]. Both anchors held while the headline reaccelerated — which is why the Bank of Canada can tolerate a brief blip above 2%[36] without flinching on the rate path.
Bank of Canada Canadian Survey of Consumer Expectations and Business Outlook Survey, Q1 2026.
