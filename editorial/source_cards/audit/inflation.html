How close is Canadian inflation to the 2% target?

Headline CPI moved above the Bank of Canada's control band in May, rising to 3.2% year-over-year[1] as energy inflation reached 22.2%[2]. The underlying signal was steadier: CPI-trim held at 2.0%[3], CPI-median held at 2.1%[4], and goods excluding energy slowed to 0.7%[5]. Breadth widened to 33.6%[6] of the basket running above 3%[7], so the print is harder to dismiss than April's, but it is still led by energy rather than a broad core re-acceleration.

Core-trim and core-median, Y/Y

03 · Breadth

Breadth widened, with 33.6%[17] of the basket running above 3%[16].

Share of CPI basket above 3% Y/Y
The valid-component breadth measure rose to 33.6% in May[17] from 28.6% in April[18], while the BoC-recipe measure rose to 34.6%[19]. That is still far below the 2022 episode when more than two thirds of the basket cleared 3%[20], but the direction is no longer benign. A narrow energy shock is becoming a broader watch item, not yet a generalized inflation episode.

Energy and goods drove the May acceleration.

Shelter, services, goods, food, energy, Y/Y
Energy inflation rose to 22.2%[21] year-over-year in May, and goods inflation rose to 4.8%[22]. The ex-energy read is much softer: goods excluding energy slowed to 0.7%[23], shelter held at 1.7%[24], services sat at 2.0%[25], and food ran at 3.8%[26]. The headline is above band, but the pressure is still concentrated in energy-sensitive goods rather than the full services/shelter core.

05 · Expectations

Expectations kept moderating as the headline jumped.

Consumer and firm inflation expectations
Five-year consumer inflation expectations in the CSCE eased to 3.0% in Q1[27], down from a recent-cycle peak of 3.7% in mid-2025[28]. On the firm side, the share of businesses expecting inflation above 3%[29] has collapsed to 11%[30], from around 40%[31] as recently as early 2024[32]. Both anchors held while the headline reaccelerated — which is why the Bank of Canada can tolerate a brief blip above 2%[33] without flinching on the rate path.
Bank of Canada Canadian Survey of Consumer Expectations and Business Outlook Survey, Q1 2026.
