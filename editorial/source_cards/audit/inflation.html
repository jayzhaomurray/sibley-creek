Section abstract

How close is Canadian inflation to the 2% target, and is the anchor holding?

Headline CPI sits at 2.3%[1] — a hair above target, with food and energy now carrying the overshoot that shelter used to. Core-trim and core-median, the measures the Bank of Canada actually reacts to, are back at 2.2-2.3%[2]. And consumer and firm expectations have moderated together: the anchor is holding while the composition rotates.

01 · Headline CPI

Headline CPI reaccelerated to 2.3% in March, the upper half of the band.

Headline CPI, m/m and Y/Y · Mar 2026
Headline inflation rose to 2.3% year-over-year in March[2] from 1.8% in February[3], a 0.5-point jump[4] that lifts CPI into the upper half of the 1-to-3% control band[5]. Prices climbed 0.5% on the month[6]. The three-month annualized run-rate sits near 2.9%[7], confirming the reacceleration is momentum, not a base effect.

02 · Preferred cores

Core measures held near target as headline reaccelerated.

Core-trim and core-median, Y/Y
Core-trim eased to 2.2% in March[1] and core-median held at 2.3%[2] — both within a tenth of the 2% target[3] and both lower than they were three months ago. The headline reaccel hasn't bled into the trimmed-mean cores, which is where the signal the Bank of Canada reads sits. Core-common, once the anchor measure, has been deprioritized in the policy frame.
Bank of Canada (CPI-trim, CPI-median, CPI-common).

03 · Breadth

Breadth widened in March: 27% of the basket runs above 3%.

Share of CPI basket above 3% Y/Y
The share of CPI components rising faster than 3% year-over-year jumped to 27.2% in March[2] from 22.3% in February[3] — a 4.9-point move[4] in a single print. That's a real broadening, not a one-line distortion. The level still sits well below the 2022 peak[5] above two thirds of the basket[6], but the direction has turned.

04 · Sub-aggregates

Composition flipped: food and energy now carry the overshoot, not shelter.

Shelter, services, goods, food, energy, Y/Y
Shelter inflation, the engine of the 2023-24 overshoot[1], has cooled to 1.7%[2] — below the 2% target[3] for a third straight month[4]. Services run at 2.5%[5] and goods at 2.1%[6], both well-behaved. The pressure has rotated to food at 4.0%[7] and energy at 3.9%[8], the two components the Bank of Canada is most willing to look through. The overshoot has migrated, not vanished.

05 · Expectations

Expectations kept moderating as the headline jumped.

Consumer and firm inflation expectations · Q1 2026 (CSCE / BOS)
Five-year consumer inflation expectations in the CSCE eased to 3.0% in Q1[1], down from a recent-cycle peak of 3.7% in mid-2025[2]. On the firm side, the share of businesses expecting inflation above 3%[3] has collapsed to 11%[4], from around 40%[5] as recently as early 2024[6]. Both anchors held while the headline reaccelerated — which is why the Bank of Canada can tolerate a brief blip above 2%[7] without flinching on the rate path.
Bank of Canada Canadian Survey of Consumer Expectations and Business Outlook Survey, Q1 2026.
