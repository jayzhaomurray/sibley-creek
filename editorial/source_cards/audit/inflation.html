How close is Canadian inflation to the 2% target?

Headline CPI fell to 2.8% year-over-year[1] in June from 3.2% in May[2], back inside the Bank of Canada's control band after a month above it. The reversal is largely energy: gasoline fell after an interim Middle East ceasefire[8], pulling energy inflation to 14.3%[3] from 22.2%[4], while CPI-trim and CPI-median eased two-tenths to 1.8%[5] and 1.9%[6], below the 2% midpoint[7]. The relief looks temporary: the ceasefire has since broken down, and oil has climbed back roughly $14 a barrel[9].

Core-trim and core-median, Y/Y

04 · Sub-aggregates

Energy's collapse is masking a pickup in services inflation.

Shelter, services, goods, food, energy, Y/Y
Energy inflation collapsed to 14.3%[24] year-over-year in June from 22.2% in May[25] as gasoline reversed, and goods inflation eased to 3.5%[26] from 4.8%[27]. Services excluding shelter accelerated to 3.0%[28] from 2.3%[29], with World Cup-related travel demand pushing hotel, rental-car, and tour prices higher in Ontario and British Columbia[30]. Shelter and food both eased slightly[31], and June's story is a rotation — energy giving back its spring, services picking up the slack — not a uniform cooling.

05 · Expectations

The inflation anchor slipped in Q2, with firms moving furthest.

Consumer and firm inflation expectations
Five-year consumer expectations rose to 3.4%[32] from 3.0%[33], and the share of firms expecting inflation above 3%[34] jumped to 44%[35] from 11%[36] — the highest reading since late 2023[37]. Both surveys were fielded in late April and May[38], as the war drove WTI crude toward $101 a barrel, up from roughly $65 before the conflict began[39], and nearly three-quarters of firms reported the war had already raised their costs[40]. June's CPI print cooled to 2.8%[41] after both surveys had closed: the backward-looking data eased while the forward-looking readings turned more worried.
Bank of Canada Canadian Survey of Consumer Expectations and Business Outlook Survey, Q2 2026.
