03 · PBO vs DoF

Two vintages of the federal deficit, converging more than they diverge.

PBO EFO Sept 2025 vs DoF SEU April 2026 deficit projection tracks
The Department of Finance projects a $66.9 billion deficit for fiscal 2025-26[10], declining to $53.2 billion by 2030-31[11]; the Parliamentary Budget Officer's September 2025⚠ outlook reads $68.5 billion in the same anchor year[12], settling near $60 billion through 2029-30[13]. The tracks differ less than the discourse suggests — assumption gaps, not policy disagreements, drive most of the wedge. Where they part is on growth elasticities and the timing of revenue normalization.
Department of Finance Spring Economic Update, April 2026; Parliamentary Budget Officer Economic and Fiscal Outlook, September 2025.

05 · Operating/capital

The PBO reclassifies $94 billion⚠ of spending the government calls capital.

Operating vs capital balance: DoF SEU Apr 2026 vs PBO Nov 2025 reclassification · November 14, 2025 (PBO Budget 2025 review)
The government's split between operating and capital treats roughly $94 billion of spending[19] over the six-year horizon as capital; the Parliamentary Budget Officer reclassifies it as operating under the international standard[21] — about 30%[20] of Ottawa's claimed capital pool. Reclassified, the operating balance is materially worse than the headline — and that gap is what the government's fiscal anchor will ultimately be judged against. The fight over what counts as capital is the fight over whether the stance is being measured honestly.
Parliamentary Budget Officer operating/capital reclassification analysis, November 2025; Department of Finance Spring Economic Update, April 2026.

06 · Debt-to-GDP

Federal debt holds above 40 per cent⚠ of GDP through the projection horizon.

Federal debt as % of GDP, 1980–2031 (historical + SEU 2026 projection) · FY2023-24 actual; projection to FY2030-31 (SEU April 2026)
The federal debt-to-GDP ratio peaked at 66.6 per cent in FY1994–95[22]; the Martin consolidation brought it to 28.2 per cent by FY2007–08[23]. The pandemic reversed that trajectory, pushing the ratio to 47.2 per cent in FY2019–20[24] before it retreated to 41.2 per cent[25] by FY2023–24. The Spring Economic Update projects a shallow peak of 41.9 per cent in FY2028–29[26], then a drift lower — declining but not converging toward pre-pandemic levels.
Department of Finance Canada, Budget 2025 Annex 1 Chart A1.5 (historical, 1980–2024); DoF Spring Economic Update April 2026, Annex 1 Table A1.7 (projection, FY2024–25 to FY2030–31).
