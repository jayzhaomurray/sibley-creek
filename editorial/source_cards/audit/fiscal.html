What is Canada's fiscal policy stance?

The fiscal stance is mildly expansionary and stays in deficit through the decade[1]—the gap narrows because program spending is held below GDP growth, not because revenue rises. Ottawa's own plan reaches an operating surplus by 2028-29[2] while booking capital investment separately as debt; record bond issuance funds the difference. Federal debt holds near 41% of GDP[3] through the forecast—well below the 66.6% of the mid-1990s[4], elevated against the past decade.

01 · Balance

Ottawa is balancing the operating books and borrowing the rest for capital.

Budget balance — full history + forecast operating/capital zoom
The plan is to balance day-to-day spending and borrow only for capital. The operating balance swings from a $26 billion⚠ deficit this year to a small surplus by 2028-29⚠; capital investment—$40⚠ to $60 billion⚠ annually, booked separately and debt-funded—holds the total in the red. The Parliamentary Budget Officer reads the split more narrowly: on its definition the operating books never reach balance, a $94 billion⚠ difference in what counts as capital. Whose accounting wins decides whether this is consolidation or a relabelling.

02 · Revenue vs Expense

Revenues and program spending converge near 15.5%⚠ of GDP—the gap is the deficit.

Federal revenues vs program expenses, % of GDP
Where program spending sits above revenue, the government runs a deficit—and spending has led for most of the past two decades. The pandemic pushed the spending line to 28%⚠ of GDP in 2020-21⚠, a spike with no peacetime precedent in the series, before it fell back; the forecast has the two converging within half a point near 15.5% of GDP by decade's end. The driver is spending held below GDP growth, not higher taxes.

04 · Debt/GDP

Federal debt has stabilized near 41%⚠ of GDP, far below its mid-1990s peak.

Federal debt, % of GDP
Canada's federal debt ratio is elevated but stable—well below its mid-1990s peak. The accumulated deficit holds near 41% of GDP across the forecast; dollar debt keeps climbing past $1.6 trillion⚠, but growing no faster than the economy keeps the ratio flat. The pandemic pushed it to 47%⚠ in 2020-21⚠—well below the 66.6%⚠ peak of the mid-1990s, before two decades of consolidation cut it to a pre-crisis low of 28%⚠. The level is elevated; the trajectory is flat.
Department of Finance Canada, Fiscal Reference Tables 2025; Spring Economic Update 2026.

Gross issuance hits a record $612 billion⚠ this fiscal year as the bond program scales up.

Debt outstanding by instrument: bonds, T-bills, retail
