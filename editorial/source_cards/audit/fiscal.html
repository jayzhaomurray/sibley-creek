02 · Debt service

Debt service is consuming a rising share of every federal revenue dollar.

Public debt charges / revenues, monthly ratio with 5-year band
Roughly ten cents of every dollar[9] of federal revenue now goes to servicing the debt, and the Parliamentary Budget Officer's projection has that share climbing past thirteen cents by 2030-31[10]. The level itself is unremarkable by historical standards; the trajectory isn't. A 2022-onward structural step-up[11] in carrying costs, compounding against a revenue base that grows with the economy rather than with rates, is what shrinks the room for fiscal response to the next downturn.
Statistics Canada Government Finance Statistics (CGFS); Parliamentary Budget Officer projections.

03 · PBO vs DoF

The watchdog and the government no longer see the same deficit track.

PBO EFO Sept 2025 vs DoF SEU April 2026 deficit projection tracks
The Department of Finance's most recent baseline projects a $66.9 billion deficit for fiscal 2025-26[12], declining to $53.2 billion by 2030-31[13]; the Parliamentary Budget Officer's September 2025⚠ outlook runs [X] billion wider over the same horizon[14]. Different growth envelopes and different revenue elasticities account for most of the gap, but the gap itself is the point: when the two official Canadian fiscal projections diverge by that much, the federal trajectory is being scored two different ways at once. The reader who anchors on only one of them is reading half the picture.

04 · Provincial debt

Provincial fiscal stance does not generalize: the spread runs from Alberta's 10.5%[17] to Quebec's 38.8%[16].

Net debt-to-GDP: ON / QC / AB / BC most-recent budgets
Ontario at 37.7%[15] and Quebec at 38.8%[16] carry the largest sub-sovereign debt loads in the country relative to the size of their economies; Alberta at 10.5%[17] sits at the other end, with British Columbia at 30.6%[18] on a taxpayer-supported basis. The dispersion is not noise — resource-revenue dependency, urban tax-base concentration, and a decade of differing fiscal restraint each pull a province into its own track. The federal fiscal stance is not the country's fiscal stance, and any read of Canadian fiscal policy that stops at Ottawa is missing the half that funds health, education, and most capital spending.
Most-recent provincial budgets: Ontario 2025-26, Quebec 2025-26, Alberta 2025-26, British Columbia 2025-26. BC figure on taxpayer-supported debt-to-GDP basis.

05 · Operating/capital

The PBO and Finance apply different definitions of capital — and the gap is not small.

Operating vs capital balance: DoF SEU Apr 2026 vs PBO Nov 2025 reclassification · Pending source card
The government's bifurcated budget treats roughly [X] billion of spending as capital[19] that the Parliamentary Budget Officer reclassifies as operating under the international-standard definition[20]. Reclassified that way, the operating balance Canadians are told about is materially worse than the headline, and the gap is not an accounting curiosity — it is the basis on which the new fiscal framework will be judged. The fight over what counts as capital is the fight over whether fiscal stance is being measured honestly at all.
Parliamentary Budget Officer operating/capital reclassification analysis, November 2025; Department of Finance Spring Economic Update, April 2026.
