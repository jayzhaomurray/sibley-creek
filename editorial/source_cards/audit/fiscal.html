Fiscal is tracking the same cadence as recent years, not leaning against the cycle. 02 · Debt service

Debt service is consuming a rising share of every federal revenue dollar.

Public debt charges / revenues, monthly ratio with 5-year band
Roughly ten cents of every federal revenue dollar[9] now goes to debt service; the Parliamentary Budget Officer projects that share rising past thirteen cents by 2030-31[10]. The current level is unremarkable historically; the trajectory isn't. A post-2022[11] structural step-up in carrying costs, compounding against a revenue base that grows with the economy rather than with rates, shrinks the room for fiscal response to the next downturn.
Statistics Canada Government Finance Statistics (CGFS); Parliamentary Budget Officer projections.

03 · PBO vs DoF

Two vintages of the federal deficit, converging more than they diverge.

PBO EFO Sept 2025 vs DoF SEU April 2026 deficit projection tracks
The Department of Finance projects a $66.9 billion deficit for fiscal 2025-26[12], declining to $53.2 billion by 2030-31[13]; the Parliamentary Budget Officer's September 2025⚠ outlook reads $68.5 billion in the same anchor year[14], settling near $60 billion through 2029-30[15]. The tracks differ less than the discourse suggests — assumption gaps, not policy disagreements, drive most of the wedge. Where they part is on growth elasticities and the timing of revenue normalisation.
Department of Finance Spring Economic Update, April 2026; Parliamentary Budget Officer Economic and Fiscal Outlook, September 2025.

04 · Provincial debt

Provincial fiscal stance does not generalize: the spread runs from Alberta's 10.5%[18] to Quebec's 38.8%[17].

Net debt-to-GDP: ON / QC / AB / BC most-recent budgets
Among the four largest provinces, Quebec at 38.8%[17] and Ontario at 37.7%[16] carry the heaviest debt-to-GDP burdens; Alberta at 10.5%[18] sits at the other end, with British Columbia at 30.6%[19] on a taxpayer-supported basis. The spread is not noise — resource-revenue dependency, urban tax-base concentration, and a decade of divergent restraint each put a province on its own track. Any read of Canadian fiscal policy that stops at Ottawa is missing the half that funds health, education, and most capital spending.

05 · Operating/capital

The PBO reclassifies $94 billion⚠ of spending the government calls capital.

Operating vs capital balance: DoF SEU Apr 2026 vs PBO Nov 2025 reclassification · November 14, 2025 (PBO Budget 2025 review)
The government's split between operating and capital treats roughly $94 billion of spending[20] over the six-year horizon as capital; the Parliamentary Budget Officer reclassifies it as operating under the international standard[22] — about 30%[21] of Ottawa's claimed capital pool. Reclassified, the operating balance Canadians see is materially worse than the headline, and the gap is the basis on which the new fiscal framework will be judged. The fight over what counts as capital is the fight over whether fiscal stance is being measured honestly.
Parliamentary Budget Officer operating/capital reclassification analysis, November 2025; Department of Finance Spring Economic Update, April 2026.
