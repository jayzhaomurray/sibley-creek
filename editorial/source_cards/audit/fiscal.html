Section abstract

What is Canada's fiscal policy stance?

The fiscal stance is mildly expansionary and stays in deficit through the decade[1]—the gap narrows because program spending is held below GDP growth, not because revenue rises. Ottawa's own plan reaches an operating surplus by 2028-29[2], funded by record bond issuance—but the Parliamentary Budget Officer says it cannot verify the surplus[3], because Ottawa has not disclosed how it sorts operating from capital[4]. Federal debt holds near 41% of GDP[5] on the government's plan; the PBO sees it drifting to 42.5% by 2030-31[6].

01 · Operating balance

Ottawa is balancing the operating books and borrowing the rest for capital.

Budget balance — full history + forecast operating/capital zoom
Ottawa's plan separates the budget in two: balance current operations, borrow only for capital. The operating balance swings from a $26 billion⚠ deficit this year to a small surplus by 2028-29⚠. Capital investment—$40⚠ to $60 billion⚠ annually, booked separately and debt-funded—holds the total in the red. So the headline deficit persists by design: the government is not aiming to balance the budget, only the operating half of it.
Department of Finance Canada, Fiscal Reference Tables 2025; Spring Economic Update 2026.

02 · Capital, disputed

Ottawa balances its operating books only on its own definition of capital.

Operating balance under two definitions of capital: DoF vs PBO · DoF Apr 2026; PBO Nov 2025
The government's fiscal anchor is a balanced operating budget by 2028-29⚠; on its own books, a small surplus that year gets it there[7]. That promise turns on what counts as capital: corporate tax breaks, investment tax credits, and operating subsidies[8] are all booked there. Re-running Budget 2025⚠ on a stricter, internationally standard definition[10], the Parliamentary Budget Officer (PBO) moved roughly $94 billion back to operating[9], where the books never balance. The government has not published those definitions as of May 2026⚠, leaving the PBO unable to verify the anchor holds[11].
Department of Finance Canada, Spring Economic Update 2026 (Annex 1, Table A1.5); Parliamentary Budget Officer, Budget 2025: Issues for Parliamentarians (RP-2526-017-S, November 2025); PBO Assessment of Spring Economic Update: Fiscal Anchors (NT-2627-002-S, May 2026).

03 · Revenue vs spending

The deficit narrows from the spending side; revenue holds.

Federal revenues vs program expenses, % of GDP
The deficit era is a spending story, not a revenue one. Revenue has hardly moved as a share of GDP—through the pandemic, which drove program spending to a peacetime-record 28%⚠ in 2020-21⚠, and through the recovery. The forecast closes the gap from the spending side, returning program expenses below revenue by decade's end and restoring the relationship that held for thirty years before 2019⚠.
Department of Finance Canada, Fiscal Reference Tables 2025; Spring Economic Update 2026.

04 · Debt/GDP

Federal debt has stabilized near 41%[12] of GDP, but Ottawa and the PBO see different paths forward.

Federal debt, % of GDP
Canada's federal debt ratio is elevated but stable—far below its mid-1990s extreme. The pandemic pushed it to 47%⚠ in 2020-21⚠, well under the 66.6%⚠ peak that more than a decade of consolidation had cut to a pre-crisis low of 28%⚠. Dollar debt keeps climbing past $1.6 trillion⚠; whether the ratio holds depends on whose forecast you take. Ottawa's plan keeps it flat near 41% through the decade[12]. The Parliamentary Budget Officer, costing the same books in June, sees it drifting to 42.5% by 2030-31[13].
Department of Finance Canada, Fiscal Reference Tables 2025; Spring Economic Update 2026; Parliamentary Budget Officer, Economic and Fiscal Outlook – June 2026 (RP-2627-002-S).

05 · Issuance by instrument

The bond program, not bills, drives issuance to a record near $612 billion⚠.

Gross issuance by maturity bucket: bills, notes, bonds
The record is a bond-program story. Issuance reaches near $612⚠ billion this fiscal year, topping the 2020-21⚠ pandemic peak, with the increase sitting almost entirely in marketable bonds: short and long tenors together climb roughly a third as larger deficits and a heavier refinancing load hit the market. The bill stock barely moves. The market absorbs the duration, not just the dollars.
Department of Finance Canada, Fiscal Reference Tables 2025; Debt Management Report (gross issuance of bonds and bills).
