01 · Overnight rate

The Bank has settled the overnight rate inside its neutral range.

Overnight rate · April 29, 2026 (rate decision)
The Bank held the overnight rate target at 2.25% on April 29[1], the fourth consecutive hold[2] since the 25 bps cut to 2.25% on October 29, 2025[3]. The setting sits inside the BoC's stated 2.25 to 3.25% nominal neutral range[4]; further easing would push policy into accommodative territory. The April Monetary Policy Report describes the current stance as appropriately positioned while the inflation moderation works through.

03 · BoC-Fed spread

The cross-border spread is deep but no longer the binding constraint on the BoC.

Canada 2y minus US 2y
GoC 2y at 2.94%[1] against UST 2y at 3.92%[2] leaves the Canada-US 2y spread at minus 98 bps[3]. On daily data since 2001[4], that reading sits at the 5th percentile of the distribution[5] -- deep, but roughly 70 bps off the all-time minus 170 bps trough[6] reached on February 3, 2025. USDCAD has held below 1.36[8] through the spring, which is why the FX channel is not currently the binding constraint on the Bank's hold.
BoC Valet (Canada 2y); FRED DGS2 (US 2y); distribution constructed.

04 · Asset composition

Government bonds anchor the post-QT balance sheet.

BoC asset composition · May 6, 2026 (weekly statement)
The Bank of Canada's asset side has settled into a shape defined by what's left of the Government Bond Purchase Programme. Government of Canada bonds account for $154.6bn of $219.0bn in total assets[1] — about 71 per cent[2] — and total assets have held near that level for roughly eighteen months, consistent with the Bank's December 2024 call that quantitative tightening was complete[3]. Repos are the operating margin, running in a $20–50bn range[4] as the Bank manages the floor system week to week, while Treasury bills and advances sit close to zero after the pandemic-era liquidity backstops ran off.
Bank of Canada weekly statement (assets and liabilities; formerly Schedule B2).

05 · Liability composition

The liability side has settled into a new floor-system equilibrium.

BoC liability composition · May 6, 2026 (weekly statement)
Banknotes carry $122.5bn of $219.0bn in total liabilities[1] — about 56 per cent[2] — and have drifted up only $30bn over six years[3], a passive function of currency demand rather than policy. Settlement balances are the active line: from a $0.25bn pre-pandemic floor[4] to a $330bn peak in 2021-22[5], drained through QT, and now $63.9bn[6] — inside the $50–70bn operating range the Bank named in its December 2024 framework statement[7]. Government of Canada deposits have normalized to $26bn[8] from a COVID spike above $80bn[9], reverse repos sit at zero, and residual other liabilities round out the remaining $7bn[10].
Bank of Canada weekly statement (assets and liabilities; formerly Schedule B2).

06 · Federal trajectory

The federal balance is tracking a familiar fiscal-year arc.

Federal budget balance, monthly and fiscal YTD · February 28, 2026 (Fiscal Monitor)
Eleven months into fiscal 2025-26[1], the federal balance is running a $25.5 billion deficit[2], after a $5.7 billion February surplus[3] pulled the year-to-date reading off its January trough of $31.2 billion[4]. The monthly panel runs deficit in eight of eleven prints and surplus in three, with the largest single-month positive landing in February as personal income tax instalments cleared. The cadence is the standard one — heavy outflows through the autumn, tax-receipt months tugging the cumulative line back toward the mean — and the next Fiscal Monitor will close the year with the March print.

07 · Policy divergence

The BoC-Fed policy gap has reached a generational depth.

BoC and Fed policy paths over time · April 2026 (BoC monthly)
BoC overnight at 2.25%[1] against the Fed's 3.75% target upper bound[2] leaves the policy-rate spread at -150 bps in monthly data through April 2026[3] -- a deeper gap than at any point in the prior two decades outside the 2002-2004 episode[4]. The bottom panel tracks the BoC-Fed spread directly: a near-zero line through most of the 1996-2019 window[5], a sharp positive spike during the 2022-23 BoC-leads-hiking phase[6], and a clean monotone descent into the current negative regime as the Bank cut ahead of the Fed through 2024-25[7]. The market-rate read of the same divergence -- the 2y GoC minus 2y UST spread, which reached -170 bps on February 3, 2025[8] -- is the deep-dive's editorial anchor; see the BoC-Fed divergence research note for the full case.
Bank of Canada Valet (overnight rate target, monthly); FRED DFF (fed funds effective, daily resampled to monthly last). BoC-Fed spread derived in chart, percentage points.
