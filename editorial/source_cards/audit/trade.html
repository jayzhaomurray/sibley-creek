Is Canada's trade pivot working?

It has started to, though gold flatters it[5]. The US took 66.6% of goods exports in July[1], 6.4 points less than a year earlier[2], as sales to other markets rose 46%[3], far faster than US-bound sales[4]. Precious metals shipped outside the US more than doubled over that year[6], and the share's fall from June owed as much to weaker US sales as to new buyers[7].

01 · Balance and US share

The US export share fell in July even as gold shipments dropped[8].

Goods trade balance (C$M) and US share of goods exports (%)
The US took 66.6% of Canada's goods exports in July[9], 6.4 points less than a year earlier[10] and close to March's low[11]. Exports to the US fell 6.5% in the month[12] while sales elsewhere reached their highest in data back to 1997[13]. March's low rode a spike in bullion shipments to the UK[14]; in July precious-metals exports fell by a fifth[15] and the share dropped anyway.

02 · Gold route

London still takes most of Canada's gold exports.

Gold and PGM exports: total and UK destination, with gold price
Canada exported C$6.8 billion of precious metals in July[16], half again as much as a year earlier[17], and the UK took 87% of it[18]. Of June's C$8.6 billion[19], the UK took two-thirds[20].

03 · Tariff-exposed sectors

Aluminum's May swing away from the US was a one-month spike.

Tariff-exposed sectors: US share of total exports, May 2025 vs May 2026 · May 2026
Aluminum's US share fell to 62.9% in May 2026 from 88.7% a year earlier[21], the only large move among five export sectors[22]. One month of unusually large shipments to other markets produced it[23], and the share had recovered to 81.5% by July[24]. From July to July, copper, aluminum, and steel each moved away from the US by roughly 6 to 9 points[25], while autos and softwood barely shifted[26].
