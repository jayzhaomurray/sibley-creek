Is Canada's trade pivot working?

Not really. The US export share dropped from three quarters to two thirds in a year[1], but most of that is gold being routed to London at record prices[2]; strip it out and the underlying share is down about three percentage points[3]. Among the tariffed sectors, only aluminum has meaningfully diversified[4] — autos lost over a billion dollars in US sales with almost no offset elsewhere[5].

03 · Gold corridor

London is buying all of Canada's gold — prices hit record highs.

Gold and PGM exports: total and UK destination, with gold price
For decades, gold has been mined in Ontario and Quebec[11] and sent straight to London's bullion banks, who store much of it in the Bank of England's vaults[12]. The story is old; what's new is the price. With spot nearly at $4,700[13], Canada sold C$8 billion of precious metals in March[14] — more than twice as much as last year[15].

04 · Tariff-exposed sectors

Of the tariff-exposed sectors, only aluminum has diversified meaningfully.

Tariff-exposed sectors: US share of total exports, March 2025 vs March 2026
Of the five tariff-exposed sectors charted[16], only aluminum has meaningfully shifted away from the US — down almost 11 points[17] between March 2025 and March 2026[18], with real volume routed to non-US buyers. Steel, softwood, and cars each shed under five points of US share[19]; copper drifted the other way and became slightly more US-dependent[20]. One sector adapted; the rest stayed locked in.

Natural Resources Canada: Ontario and Quebec are the two largest gold-producing provinces, together accounting for roughly three-quarters of Canadian gold production.
[12] Derived (arithmetic from other tagged claims)
"sent straight to London's bullion banks, who store much of it in the Bank of England's vaults"
Royal Canadian Mint named on the original LBMA Good Delivery List in 1934; Canadian refined gold flows to LBMA-member bullion banks (HSBC, JPM, ICBC Standard) in London, who custody a substantial portion of their bullion at the Bank of England. BoE vaults hold ~5,000 tonnes of gold, of which only ~300 tonnes is UK reserves; the balance is custody for foreign central banks and commercial bullion banks.

Chart renders five sectors: steel (NAPCS 30+31), aluminum (NAPCS 32+38), copper (NAPCS 33), softwood (NAPCS 55), cars (NAPCS 81). Of these, steel/aluminum/copper/cars are subject to US Section 232 tariffs (steel + aluminum 2018+, copper July 2025 50%, autos April 2025 25%); softwood is covered by US AD/CVD duties (Lumber V dispute, 2017+) rather than Section 232. 'Tariff-exposed' is the accurate framing. Source: StatCan 12-10-0182-01.
