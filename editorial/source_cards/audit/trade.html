Is Canada's trade pivot working?

The goods trade balance narrowed to -$2.2B on a 3mma basis in March[1], per Statistics Canada, an $876M improvement from February[2]. The US export share fell 2.5pp to 66.1%[3], the lowest reading in the available window and a continuation of the year-long drift down from the 76 to 80% range that held through 2024[4]. The Q4 2025 current account narrowed to -$706M[5], a $4.6B improvement[6]; terms of trade ticked up 0.6 to 105.5.

01 · US export share

Canadian exports are reorienting away from the US at the fastest peacetime pace in decades.

US export share of total goods exports
The US absorbed 66.1% of Canadian goods exports in March 2026[7], down from 74.4% in March 2025[8] and a 2024 calendar average near 76.3%[9]. Total exports rose to a record $68.8 billion[10] over the same window, so the share decline reflects geographic redirection rather than an export contraction. A ten-percentage-point move in fourteen months[11] is the largest peacetime reorientation of Canadian trade in decades.

02 · Sector diagnostic

Only one of the four tariff-exposed sectors has actually pivoted.

Tariff-exposed sectors: US vs non-US exports, 12mma indexed Jan 2020 = 100
Indexed to January 2020[12], the non-US 12-month moving average sits at 236 for aluminum[13], 226 for softwood[15], 202 for steel[14], and 116 for autos[16], but only aluminum's pivot represents real reorientation in dollar terms. Steel's non-US index reads at 82 with US at 86[17], both below baseline — the sector is shrinking on both sides, not pivoting. Softwood non-US has collapsed to 68 while US sits at 110[18], the inverse of a pivot. Autos non-US rose to 115 from baseline but US flows are roughly five times larger[19], so the relative shift is small in absolute terms.

03 · Gold corridor

The UK takes 97%⚠ of Canadian gold — but the gold price is doing the work.

Gold and PGM exports: total and UK destination, with gold price
Canadian gold and platinum-group-metal exports hit C$8.0 billion in March 2026[20], of which C$7.8 billion (97%) went to the UK[21]. Volume rose roughly 8% year-on-year through September 2025[22]; export value rose 58%[23] — the corridor is the same architecture it's been since 1934[25], amplified by a gold price near a record $4,676 per ounce[24]. Canadian mined gold gets refined into 400-ounce Good Delivery bars and physically shipped to London because LBMA accreditation requires it. The UK destination is a wholesale market routing, not an industrial buyer.

04 · Aluminum corridor

Non-US aluminum is essentially one port hub.

Aluminum exports by destination, 12mma
Canadian aluminum exports run at a 12-month average of C$772 million to the US[26] and C$141 million to the Netherlands[27] — Rotterdam alone carries 77% of all non-US aluminum flow[28]. Mexico, the next-largest non-US destination, takes only C$22 million[29]; every other partner is under C$2 million[30]. The Rotterdam corridor is real but narrow: it's tariff-arbitrage flow that emerged in 2025[31] when the US 50% Section 232 rate[32] made the Atlantic crossing pencil out. It works while the tariff holds. One negotiated rollback and the corridor reverses.
