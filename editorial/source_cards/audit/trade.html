Is Canada's trade pivot working?

Not really. The trade surplus widened again in May[1], but the US export share is back at 70.0%[2] and looks little changed from a year ago[3]. The apparent diversification pulse was still mostly gold routed to London[4]; that flow is cooling[5], and among tariff-exposed sectors aluminum is the only clear shift away from the US[6] while copper leaned more heavily toward it[7].

The US export share is back near 70%[10].

Goods trade balance (C$M) and US share of goods exports (%)
Canada's US-bound export share fell to 66.0% in March[8], then rebounded to 69.2% in April[9] and 70.0% in May[10]. That is close to where it was a year earlier[11]. The apparent diversification pulse was mostly gold: shipments to London surged, then cooled[12] as the gold flow normalized.

Trade diversification is extremely limited.

Δ exports to US vs Δ exports to non-US, by NAPCS sub-chapter, May 2025 to May 2026
Gold still accounts for the clearest non-US export growth[13], but the surge is fading: total precious-metals exports fell from C$8.0B in March to C$5.6B in May[14]. 03 · Gold corridor

London is still buying most of Canada's gold, but the surge is cooling.

Gold and PGM exports: total and UK destination, with gold price
For decades, gold has been mined in Ontario and Quebec[16] and sent straight to London's bullion banks, who store much of it in the Bank of England's vaults[17]. The story is old; what changed was the scale of the flow. Canada sold C$5.6 billion of precious metals in May[18], down from C$8.0 billion in March[19], and the UK's share fell to 83% from 97%[20].

04 · Tariff-exposed sectors

Aluminum is the only clear move away from the US.

Tariff-exposed sectors: US share of total exports, May 2025 vs May 2026
Of the tariff-exposed sectors charted[22], aluminum is the only clear move away from the US[23] between May 2025 and May 2026[21]. Its US share fell from 88.7% to 62.9%[24], while steel, softwood, and autos each moved away by less than five points[25] and copper moved further toward the US[26]. One sector is moving; the rest are still mostly tied to the US market.

"sent straight to London's bullion banks, who store much of it in the Bank of England's vaults"
Royal Canadian Mint named on the original LBMA Good Delivery List in 1934; Canadian refined gold flows to LBMA-member bullion banks (HSBC, JPM, ICBC Standard) in London, who custody a substantial portion of their bullion at the Bank of England. BoE vaults hold ~5,000 tonnes of gold, of which only ~300 tonnes is UK reserves; the balance is custody for foreign central banks and commercial bullion banks.
