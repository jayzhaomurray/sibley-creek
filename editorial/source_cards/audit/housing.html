03 · Inventory

Absorption is soft but has not tipped into a buyers' market.

Sales-to-new-listings ratio, Canada
The national sales-to-new-listings ratio sits at 47.6 in February 2026[17], up from 46.4 in January[18] but just below the 45-to-65 band CREA describes as balanced[19]. The resale index has eased to 85.6[20], leaving Canada in a soft absorption regime rather than an outright buyers' market. CMA-level 12-month resale counts to October 2025[21] point in the same direction: Toronto and Vancouver are absorbing the most volume relative to their listings overhang.

04 · Affordability

Affordability is improving from peak but remains worse than prior stress episodes.

BoC qualifying mortgage payment to income
The BoC's qualifying-mortgage-payment-to-income ratio sits at 42.7% in Q4 2025[22], down from 43.2% in Q3[23] and 54.5% at the Q3 2023 peak[24]. The improvement traces to lower qualifying rates — the 5-year conventional has settled at 6.09%[25] — partly offset by softer wage growth in the denominator. The index remains well above the 1989-1991[26] and 2007-2008[27] stress episodes; the 2022-2024 tightening cycle[28] is still the binding historical anchor.
Bank of Canada Valet (INDINF_AFFORD_Q, housing affordability index, qualifying payment / income).

05 · Stress

Mortgage stress is contained.

Mortgage arrears, household debt-service ratio · Feb 2026 (arrears) / 2025 Q4 (DSR)
Realized distress remains contained: the bank mortgage arrears rate sits at 0.28% in February[29], well within its historical range and far below stress-episode peaks. The aggregate household debt-service ratio at 14.57%[30] has eased from the 2024 cycle high[31] as the rate-cut cycle flowed through to renewing borrowers. Households have absorbed the tightening cycle without breaking; the housing system isn't accelerating into a boom or cracking into a bust.
Canadian Bankers Association mortgage arrears; Statistics Canada Table 11-10-0065-01 (household debt-service ratio).
