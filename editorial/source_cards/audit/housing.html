03 · Inventory

Absorption is soft but has not tipped into a buyers' market.

Sales-to-new-listings ratio, Canada
The national sales-to-new-listings ratio sits at 47.6 in February 2026[1], up from 46.4 in January[2] but below the 50-to-70 band CREA describes as balanced[3]. The resale index has eased to 85.6[4], leaving Canada in a soft absorption regime rather than an outright buyers' market. CMA-level 12-month resale counts to October 2025[5] point in the same direction: Toronto and Vancouver are absorbing the most volume relative to their listings overhang.

07 · Affordability

Affordability is improving from peak but remains worse than prior stress episodes.

BoC qualifying mortgage payment to income
The BoC's qualifying-mortgage-payment-to-income ratio sits at 42.7% in Q4 2025[1], down from 43.2% in Q3[2] and 50.4% at the Q2 2024 peak[3]. The improvement traces to lower qualifying rates — the 5-year conventional has settled at 6.09%[4] — partly offset by softer wage growth in the denominator. The index remains well above the 1989-1991[5] and 2007-2008[6] stress episodes; the 2022-2024 tightening cycle[7] is still the binding historical anchor.
Bank of Canada Valet (INDINF_AFFORD_Q, housing affordability index, qualifying payment / income).
