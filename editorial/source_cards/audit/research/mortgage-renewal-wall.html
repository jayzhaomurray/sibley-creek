The mortgage renewal wall has peaked

The 2026 renewal cohort is the largest single tranche in the stack. We map where the residual transmission lands through 2027.

The mortgage renewal wall has peaked. The fixed-rate stock booked at 2020-21[28] contract rates -- the cohort that carried the bulk of the renewal-payment-shock distribution -- has now mostly rolled, and the mechanical pulse on household cash flows has begun to fade. Mortgage interest cost in the CPI tells the story most cleanly: at 0.28% Y/Y in March 2026[2], its contribution to inflation is essentially extinguished, after running near double digits at the 2023[3]-2024[3] peak.

The BoC held the overnight rate at 2.25% on April 29, 2026[6] -- the fourth consecutive hold since the October 29, 2025 cut[7]. The floor on the renewal-rate spread is now stable; the residual transmission is about the height and persistence of that floor, not about how much further it falls.

Peaked is not over, and what has peaked is narrower than the renewal-wall framing of 2024 implied. The BoC's Staff Analytical Paper 2026-12⚠ (March 24, 2026[68]) decomposes the historical drivers of Canadian mortgage arrears into a labour-market channel and a rate channel, and finds the labour market is the central force. The implied incremental arrears from a +300 bps⚠ tightening cycle is 10 to 15 bps⚠ over two years; each 1 pp⚠ rise in the unemployment rate adds roughly 10 bps⚠ to the arrears rate within a year. The 2022[14]-2023 tightening has now mostly passed through the arrears series. The forward risk is no longer the renewal wall.

This piece argues three things. First, the count of renewers facing a step-up in payment has peaked and the rate-channel pass-through is largely realized. Second, the household-side response has rotated from amortization extensions to discretionary-consumption compression -- a slower-burn channel that does not show up in any single monthly print. Third, the forward residual arrears risk has rotated with it: from the renewal-cohort tail, where the marginal incremental damage is small, to the labour-market channel, where the marginal lever is roughly 2 to 3 times larger per unit of shock[15].

---

The rate channel and the labour channel

BoC SAP 2026-12 quantifies the rate channel and the labour channel as separate drivers of Canadian mortgage arrears: a full +300 bps tightening cycle adds 10 to 15 bps to the arrears rate over two years; a +1 pp rise in unemployment adds roughly 10 bps within a year. The 2022-2023 tightening has now mostly transmitted -- the observed +8 bps⚠ move from the mid-2022 trough (0.14%[66]) to the Q2 2025⚠ reading (0.22%[66]) sits inside the 10 to 15 bps range. The marginal lever from the labour channel is comparable to the entire 2022-2023 tightening on a per-pp basis. Canadian mortgage arrears are now mostly a labour-market story on the forward path; the renewal-wall mechanic is no longer the frame that anchors forward credit risk.

---

The stock

Federally-regulated banks hold C$1,952 billion of residential mortgage debt as of February 28, 2026, per OSFI's M4 Consolidated Balance Sheet: C$433 billion insured, C$1,508 billion uninsured, C$11 billion reverse. The all-lender total reported by CMHC for Q2 2025[7] is C$2.3 trillion, so the chartered-bank book is approximately 84%[22] of the total, with credit unions, MICs, and other non-federally- regulated lenders carrying the remaining 16%[23]. Y/Y growth in the chartered-bank uninsured book is +1.5%⚠ (from C$1,484 billion in February 2025⚠); the insured book is -0.2%⚠ Y/Y. The Big-Six aggregate mortgage book sits at roughly C$1.76 trillion per Q1 FY2026 institutional filings.

Within that stock, five-year fixed remains the dominant contract. BoC Staff Analytical Note 2025-1 (January 2025⚠; data vintage September 2024⚠) reports fixed-rate at roughly 80%⚠ of the stock and variable-rate at roughly 20%⚠; five-year fixed alone is about 40%[27] of the total. The cohorts that framed the renewal-wall narrative since 2023 are those originated near the policy-rate floor of the 2020-21 pandemic window.

The renewal schedule from here is the variable that matters. Per BoC Staff Analytical Note 2025-21[34] (July 2025⚠), 60%⚠ of all outstanding mortgages renew during 2025 or 2026. CMHC Residential Mortgage Industry Report Fall 2025 puts roughly 1.15 million⚠ households renewing in 2026 specifically; CMHC's broader January 2026⚠ framing places about 3.1 million⚠ mortgages (52%⚠ of total) renewing during 2026. The rolling-cohort distribution implies approximately 20 to 30%[44] of stock rolls in 2027⚠[33]. After 2027, the wave smooths.

---

The stress

Payment shock at renewal is the cohort change in scheduled monthly mortgage payment when a contract resets, holding amortization constant. BoC SAN 2025-21 frames this as a distribution[34] rather than a single number: the median renewer's experience differs sharply from the tail's. The BoC's preferred denominator is the Mortgage Debt Service ratio -- mortgage-only debt service as a share of gross income -- not full DSR.

The central estimate has been revised lower as rates have fallen. The BoC's 2024-vintage central case put the median 5-year fixed renewer in the 30 to 40% payment-increase band; that vintage is now outdated. The most recent BoC work (FSR May 2025⚠; SAN 2025-21, July 2025) places the central estimate at 15 to 20% for 5-year fixed renewers in 2025 or 2026, and 6 to 10%[37] across all renewing mortgages. Average monthly payment for 2025 renewers is roughly 10% above the pre-renewal level; for 2026 renewers, roughly 6%⚠. Across payment increasers, median MDS rises 2.7 pp⚠ (15.3%⚠ to 18.0%⚠); among payment decreasers -- primarily short-term fixed and some variable-rate cohorts -- median MDS falls 1.1 pp⚠ (19.7%⚠ to 18.6%⚠).

The distribution is the read that matters. The top 10% of 2026 renewers face a payment increase of more than 40%; the bottom 25%⚠ see at least a 7%⚠ decrease. Five-year fixed mortgages account for roughly 40% of all outstanding mortgages but about 75%⚠ of those facing payment increases at renewal. About one-third of all Canadian mortgage holders will see payment increases by end-2026; close to 25% see decreases. CIBC disclosed in its Q1 FY2026 earnings supplement that roughly 6% of its mortgage portfolio faces a payment shock of 40% or more in 2026 -- a clean read on the tail's concentration at one chartered bank.

Three characteristics define the residual tail: original amortizations at or near 30 years (a feature of 2020-21 originations in expensive markets, not a fluke), uninsured loan- to-value ratios that leave little room to refinance into longer- amortization products without re-qualification under tighter B-20 stress-test conventions, and concentration in the Toronto and Vancouver CMAs where original nominal loan sizes were largest. CMHC Residential Mortgage Industry Report Fall 2025 reports the flow share of new uninsured mortgages at chartered banks with amortizations longer than 25 years remained above 60% in Q2 2025⚠ for the fourth consecutive year.

---

The response and the channel

The consumer-side response has rotated. In 2023-2024, the dominant margin of adjustment was structural: lenders extended amortizations -- in some cases past the contractual maximum at the cost of a future re-amortization at re-qualification -- to spread the renewal shock across a longer schedule. That margin has now largely been used. The current margin of adjustment is discretionary consumption.

StatCan's quarterly expenditure cut shows household consumption at +0.4%[66] Q/Q in Q4 2025⚠ (+2.3%⚠ on the year), with the household saving rate at 4.4%[66] in Q4 2025 and 4.9%[66] for 2025 annual. Both numbers sit inside the post-COVID range: aggregate consumption is positive but soft, and the saving rate is normalizing downward from the 2023-2024 highs as households absorb the cash-flow imprint of higher debt service. Q1 2026⚠ retail-trade prints confirm the shape: core retail ran +0.9%[66] M/M in January and +0.6%[66] M/M in February; March's advance estimate is +0.6% M/M. Positive but not strong. The renewal channel does not show up as a single month's collapse in retail sales; it shows up as a multi-quarter drag on the discretionary-services side, partly offsetting the mechanical fade of mortgage interest cost in the inflation print.

SAP 2026-12's section heading is direct: "The labour market is the key driver of arrears." The arrears rate ran at 0.2%[66] before the pandemic and reached 0.5%[66] at the GFC peak; SAP 2026-12 frames forward stress as potentially surpassing the GFC peak but not the 1990s peaks, conditional on the labour market. House price growth, core inflation, and the output gap are less significant contributors.

CBA's chartered-bank monthly arrears series for February 2026⚠ reads 0.28% nationally (13,749 mortgages in arrears out of 4,937,235 covered) -- up from the 0.14% mid-2022 trough[54]. The +14 bps move sits inside the SAP 2026-12 range[55] for the realized rate-channel pass-through; the level remains well below the 0.5% GFC peak. Q1 FY2026 chartered-bank filings show combined writeoffs of C$38 million on the C$1.76 trillion combined mortgage portfolio[25]; trailing four quarters, C$168 million in writeoffs, equivalent to 0.01% of holdings[58]. The build is real but concentrated; the system-level loss read remains near a cyclical floor.

The remaining renewal-rate spread for the 2026H2 and 2027 cohorts is materially narrower than the 2024-2025 cohorts faced -- perhaps a quarter of the size, with the BoC having cut 275 bps⚠ from the mid-2024 peak and held at 2.25%⚠ for four meetings. The residual incremental rate-channel arrears contribution is therefore small. What matters for the next leg is whether the labour market turns.

---

What would change our view

Three triggers fire against this view; the falsification thresholds have been re-cast around the labour channel as the larger forward lever.

The wall peaks earlier than this piece claims if: CMHC's mortgage arrears rate (90+ days) stalls or rolls over within the next two Residential Mortgage Industry Report releases; CBA's chartered-bank monthly arrears falls below 0.25%[66] for three consecutive months from the current 0.28% reading; and Big-Six writeoffs on residential mortgages trail below the current C$168 million four-quarter baseline[59] in the Q2 FY2026 earnings cycle. The combination would say the 2024-2025 stress has begun to dissipate faster than the cohort-mechanics model predicts.

The wall extends past 2027 if: Canadian unemployment moves above 7.5%[61] on a sustained basis (two consecutive quarters), or the Labour Force Survey prints back-to-back monthly increases of 0.2 pp or more[62]. This is the central trigger after the SAP 2026-12 reframe: a +1 pp rise in unemployment adds roughly 10 bps to the arrears rate within a year, comparable in magnitude to the entirety of the 2022-2023 rate-channel pass-through. The April 2026⚠ LFS print has unemployment at 6.9%⚠; a 50 bps move above that[64], sustained, puts the arrears trajectory on a path worse than renewal-channel modelling alone predicts. CMHC arrears continuing to rise through 2026H2 and into 2027 -- with the Toronto sub-aggregate moving above CMHC's own Q4 2026⚠ projection of 0.34%[66] -- would be supporting. A 5-year GoC yield above 4.0%[66] would re-widen the renewal-rate spread, but the marginal arrears impact is small relative to the labour channel.

The cheapest test that would resolve the labour-channel reframe is the next LFS release (June 6, 2026 for May 2026 data)[67]. Labour- market deterioration shows up in the LFS first, in the CBA arrears series with roughly a one-year lag per SAP 2026-12. If the next LFS holds unemployment at or below 6.9% and the Spring 2026 Residential Mortgage Industry Report[68] shows the regional arrears geography stable, the renewal-wall residual is the backward-looking story this piece claims it is. If the LFS prints a 50 bps[64] deterioration, the forward read flips to the labour channel even faster than this piece anticipates.
