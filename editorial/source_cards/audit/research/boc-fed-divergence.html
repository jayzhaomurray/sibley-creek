The BoC-Fed divergence is wide, but FX is not binding

The policy spread sits at the 8th percentile of three decades; USDCAD is at the 67th and strengthening. The binding constraint is not the loonie but the expectations chain.

The Bank of Canada's overnight rate target sits at 2.25%[1], where it has held for four consecutive meetings since October 29, 2025[2]. The Federal Reserve's policy rate sits at 3.75%[3], the upper bound of its 3.50 to 3.75% target range last reset by a 25 bps⚠ cut in December 2025⚠. The BoC-Fed policy-rate spread is therefore negative 150 bps[5]. In the post-1996 monthly distribution of that spread[6], today's reading sits near the post-1996[6] floor. At the 2y point, GoC yields 2.94%[7] against UST at 3.92%[8]; the 98 bps⚠ gap sits at the 5th percentile of the post-2001[24] daily distribution. This is a deep- divergence episode by any sensible measure.

The naive transmission story -- wider negative rate spread, weaker loonie, imported goods inflation, BoC capitulation -- is not playing out. USDCAD closed May 1 at 1.3575[10], the 67th percentile of the post-1990[11] daily distribution; the loonie has strengthened through spring 2026[20], from above 1.40 earlier in the cycle down to roughly 1.36. The BoC's April 29 statement[13] noted, in characteristic understatement, that "the Canada-US exchange rate has been relatively stable." That the spread can sit near the post-1996 floor while the currency sits at the 67th percentile of its post-1990 distribution is the most interesting datum in the Canadian macro picture today: broad USD weakness against the major DM crosses and resilient commodity terms of trade have pulled against the rate differential with at least as much force.

The argument is in three parts. First, the FX channel is not the binding constraint on the BoC right now -- the Bank itself is not flagging it, and the 2024-25[32] episode demonstrated that BoC can tolerate a -170 bps⚠ 2y spread without capitulating. Second, the binding constraint is the inflation-expectations chain -- the consumer 1y and 5y Canadian Survey of Consumer Expectations measures and the Business Outlook Survey firm distribution -- and that chain is currently moderating toward target, not breaking. Third, the 2024[19]-25 episode is the operating precedent, not 1997-98⚠; the resolution then came from Fed convergence, not BoC capitulation, and the structural setup for a repeat is already in place.

Where the divergence stands

The 2y curve spread is the sharper read of market-implied policy divergence. Its post-2001 all-time minimum was negative 170 bps⚠ on February 3, 2025, the trough of the 2024-25 episode that this piece argues is the operating precedent. Today's minus 98 bps is near that floor, roughly 70 bps⚠ off it. Deep but not unprecedented.

Why the FX channel is not binding

The textbook story is mechanical: wider negative rate spread, capital outflow pressure, weaker loonie, higher Canadian-dollar price of imported goods, lift in headline CPI, BoC forced to back off. This is not happening. The data say so, and the BoC says so.

USDCAD at 1.3575 is at the 67th percentile of the post-1990 daily distribution. The loonie strengthened through the first four months of 2026 as the BoC-Fed policy spread widened. The two series moved in opposite directions to what the textbook would predict. The BoC's April 29 statement called the exchange rate "relatively stable" -- a statement the Bank would not make if it considered FX a binding constraint on policy.

Two channels are dominating the rate differential through spring 2026. The first is broad USD weakness: the DXY has been on the back foot through Q1 and into Q2, and when the dollar is leaking against majors broadly, the loonie tends to participate even when the bilateral rate differential argues against it. The second is commodity terms of trade -- WTI has been firm through Q1 and Q2[31], and Canada's terms-of-trade position has been supportive. Both channels pulled in the same direction against the rate differential, and the net on USDCAD was a strengthening currency.

For the pass-through loop to bind on CPI, the currency move must be material in size and durable in time. Today's CAD is broadly flat year-to-date. The loop is dormant.

The FX channel can be not binding today and still be the channel that binds tomorrow. The threshold is not the spread level; it is the CAD level. The 2024-25 precedent saw USDCAD touch 1.45-plus without forcing a back-off. Today's 1.36 sits well inside the range[33] in which the BoC has historically tolerated a wide negative spread.

What is binding instead

The Bank's reaction function in this cycle has not been keyed to the rate spread or the FX level. It has been keyed to the inflation-expectations chain: the CSCE consumer 1y and 5y series, the BOS distribution of firms expecting CPI above 3%[36], and the headline CPI print. As long as those move in the right direction, the Bank has runway to hold at 2.25%[37] and tolerate the spread.

CSCE consumer 1y expectations sat at 3.98% in Q1 2026[38], down from 4.10%⚠ in Q4 2025⚠ and 4.0% in Q3 2025[40]. CSCE consumer 5y expectations sat at 3.02%[36] in Q1 2026⚠, down from 3.09% in Q4 2025[42] and 3.67%[36] in Q3 2025⚠ -- a move of more than 60 bps over two quarters[44], now within touching distance of a 2-handle reading. The BOS share of firms expecting CPI above 3%[36] sat at 11%⚠ in Q1 2026, down from 16%⚠ in Q4 2025⚠ and 18% in Q3 2025[47]. All three series are moderating; none are breaking. The composition is consumer-led and firm-confirmed -- the configuration that makes the BoC most confident an expectations process is genuinely re-anchoring.

Headline CPI cooperates. The March 2026⚠ print placed headline CPI Y/Y at 2.32%⚠. The BoC's April 29 statement projects CPI rising to "about 3%" in April on a gasoline base effect, then declining toward 2%⚠ by early 2027⚠. If April lands at or below the Bank's guide and subsequent prints decline, the expectations framework holds. If April prints materially above 3% and the decline does not arrive, that is the first test.

The Bank does not target the rate spread or USDCAD; it targets headline CPI at 2% over the medium term, with attention to whether expectations are anchored there. When expectations are anchored and CPI is on a path to 2%, the Bank can absorb a wide negative rate spread -- the channel through which the spread would otherwise bind (FX-to-import-prices-to-CPI) requires expectations to de-anchor for the second-round effects to matter. Today they are not.

The 2024-25 precedent

The 2024-25 episode is the right structural analogue, not the 1996-98[54] capitulation cycle. The BoC was ahead of the Fed on cuts: BoC's first landed June 2024[53], three months before the Fed's first in September 2024[55]. The 2y GoC- UST spread reached its post-2001 minimum of negative 170 bps on February 3, 2025. USDCAD touched 1.45-plus intraday in late Q1 2025[57] without forcing a BoC reversal -- a direct test of the threshold question. The resolution came from Fed convergence: the Fed cut into late 2025; the BoC's final cut brought the overnight target to 2.25%[1] on October 29, 2025; the 2y spread closed from minus 170 bps to the minus 98 bps reading today. The BoC did not reverse. The Fed caught up. Canadian inflation expectations were anchored throughout, and the FX move was uncomfortable but not catastrophic.

If the 2024-25 precedent holds, the binding sequence in 2026 is not "BoC capitulates to close the spread"; it is "the Fed eventually cuts and the spread compresses passively." The next FOMC meeting is the active variable; the BoC's June 10 meeting[61] is, on the signalled path, more likely a hold than a cut. The Fed's path is the swing factor.

What would change our mind

If USDCAD breaks 1.45 and holds for a full quarter[66], the FX channel re-activates as the binding constraint. The 2024-25 episode established that intraday 1.45-plus does not force a back-off; a sustained quarter at or above that level would. Today's 1.3575 is roughly 7%[67] weaker than the trigger.

If CSCE 5y consumer expectations re-accelerate to 3.5%-plus[68], the expectations anchor is in question and the BoC's runway shortens materially. The 5y measure was at 3.67% in Q3 2025 and has moderated to 3.02% in Q1 2026. A reversal above 3.5%[36] in Q2 or Q3 2026⚠ would mark an expectations process that has stopped re- anchoring. The Q2 release in July 2026 is the next watchpoint[71].

If headline CPI prints above 3% in April 2026[72] and the May or June print fails to decline, the BoC's own forward path is falsified. April is the test of the gasoline-base-effect framing; May and June are the test of the decline. A failure to decline would indicate broader inflation pressure than the gasoline story explains.

If credit conditions tighten sharply -- Canadian investment-grade / high-yield spreads widening 50 bps[74]-plus, bank-funding spreads widening, or mortgage- GoC spreads widening -- the credit channel binds even if FX and expectations do not.

The base case: the BoC holds at 2.25% through 2026[75] and the spread compresses passively as the Fed cuts; the 2024-25 precedent reruns, in shape if not in sequence. The binding variable is the Fed's path, not the Bank's. The single piece of data that would update this call fastest is the Q2 2026⚠ CSCE 5y consumer expectations print, released July 2026⚠: below 2.75% extends the runway[77], above 3.25%[36] breaks the chain. The leading indicator to watch is the expectations chain, not the currency.
