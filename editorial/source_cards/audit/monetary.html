What is Canada's monetary policy stance?

On hold. The Bank of Canada has stayed at 2.25%[1] through six straight decisions[2], still at the floor of its 2.25 to 3.25% neutral range[3], and now calls the rate appropriate[4]. Markets see it the same way: the 2-year GoC yield sits at 3.02%[5], 77 bps above the overnight rate[6], while the Canada-US 2-year spread is still deeply negative at -117 bps[7].

02 · Policy divergence

The BoC-Fed policy gap is narrowing from a generational depth.

BoC and Fed policy paths over time · Jun 2026
The BoC-Fed gap stands at -150 bps[16], narrowest since February 2025[17] and 25 bps off the -175 trough[18] sustained from March through November 2025[19], when the BoC out-cut a Fed already a full point into its own easing[20]. The market still isn't pricing a near-term cut.

2y GoC vs overnight

04 · Asset composition

Government bonds anchor the post-QT balance sheet.

BoC asset composition
The asset side has settled into a shape defined by what's left of the pandemic-era bond-purchase programme. Government of Canada bonds account for $146.0 billion of $220.0 billion in total assets[28] — about 66%[29] — easing lower as the portfolio matures while the total holds steady. Quantitative tightening is complete[30]; repos run in a roughly $15 to 40 billion range[31] as the operating margin of the floor system.

05 · Liability composition

The overnight rate is now the only active policy lever.

BoC liability composition
Banknotes carry $123.9 billion of $220.0 billion in total liabilities[32] — about 56%[33] — drifting up only $30 billion over six years[34] as a passive function of currency demand. Settlement balances sit at $64.3 billion[35], inside the $50 to 70 billion operating range[36] and a small fraction of the near-$400 billion peak in early 2021[37]. Reverse repos are at zero[38] — none of it is doing policy work.
