How fast is Canada's economy growing?

Slowly, and mostly in the resource patch. Real GDP is running near 1% Y/Y[1], just below the Bank of Canada's 1.2% estimate of near-term potential growth[2]. Oil and gas are doing the heavy lifting while manufacturing is in deep recession. Services growth is trundling along. Even that 1%[1] pace relies on population growth more than anything — on a per capita basis, output is scarcely higher than it was before the pandemic.

02 · Contributions

Inventories drag the Q4 headline below the underlying pulse.

Contributions to quarterly growth, by bucket
Real GDP contracted at a 0.6% annualized pace in Q4 2025[7], but the underlying composition is more constructive than the headline. Final domestic demand added 2.3 percentage points[8] and net trade another 1.5[9], while a 4.2-point destocking[10] did the damage. The quarter's weakness is more about how much was produced for the shelf than how much was sold.

03 · Industry composite

Resources have outrun factories by 18 percentage points[12] since January 2023[11].

Real GDP by industry, services vs goods Y/Y + manufacturing vs mining and oil indexed to Jan 2023 · Feb 2026
Mining and oil-and-gas output is up roughly 12%[13] since January 2023[11]; manufacturing is down about 6%[14] — an 18-point split[15] inside the goods economy. Services, which employ most Canadians, are running at about 1% year-over-year[16]. The divergence maps cleanly onto Canada's regional economic geography: resource provinces pulling one direction, central-Canada factory floors pulling the other.

04 · Output gap

The output gap reopened to 1%[17] in Q4 — a hold story, not a hike one.

BoC MPR output gap · 2025Q4; April 2026 MPR vintage
The Bank of Canada's output gap estimate widened to -1.0% in Q4[17] from -0.5% in Q3[18], the deepest reading since early 2021[19]. The shortfall is demand-side: not enough spending to absorb the economy's productive capacity. A gap this size keeps the easing bias intact and takes a near-term hike off the conversation.
Bank of Canada Monetary Policy Report, April 2026 (Valet INDINF_OUTGAPMPR_Q).

05 · Per capita

Total output is up about 11%[21] since the pre-pandemic peak — per-capita output is barely above it.

Total vs per-capita real GDP, indexed to pre-pandemic baseline (Q4 2019)
Total real GDP has grown about 11%[21] since Q4 2019[20]; on a per-person basis, output has risen less than 1%[22]. The 10-point wedge[23] is the population story — Canada has added nearly 3.8 million people[24] in that span, enough to absorb almost all the headline growth without lifting living standards.
