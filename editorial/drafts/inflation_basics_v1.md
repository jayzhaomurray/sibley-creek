## 1. Section header headline question

The EDR-canonical question (Section 4.2) is:

> *Is the BoC's 2% target being met, and on what measures and what
> breadth?*

This is locked in canon and reproduced verbatim. No polish proposed. The
question carries three independent reads: position relative to target
(panels 1 and 2), the measure on which it is met (panel 2), and the
breadth of the distribution that sits behind the headline (panel 3). A
polish that dropped any clause would make the page header narrower than
the page.

## 2. Page lede

Three sentences, set immediately below the page-level as-of stamp. State
of inflation today, anchored to the March 2026 print; no editorializing
on direction beyond what the panels and the BoC's own April 2026 MPR
support.

> Headline CPI rose 2.4% year-over-year in March, just above the Bank of
> Canada's 2% target, with the BoC's preferred core measures at 2.2%
> (trim) and 2.3% (median) -- both close to target on a re-anchored
> trajectory. The composition has rotated: shelter has cooled to 1.7%
> Y/Y as mortgage-interest cost is essentially extinguished, while
> energy at 3.9% and food at 4.0% are now the marginal pressure points.
> Roughly 44% of the basket by weight is running below 1% Y/Y and 27%
> above 3% -- a wide distribution, not a clustered one.

Voice notes:
- Mode-B-adjacent register: short, declarative, no "we think." No
  "Goldilocks," no "soft landing," no "the consumer."
- The lede answers the headline question across its three clauses:
  position vs target (headline + core), composition rotation (shelter
  out, energy/food in), and breadth (the 44/29/27 split). Panels then
  expand each.
- "Re-anchored" is the load-bearing word. It is grounded in the trim and
  median trajectory (3.0-3.1% mid-2025 to 2.2-2.3% March 2026, per
  research-pack Panel 2) and the BOS firms-expecting->3% trajectory
  (84% Q4 2022 to 11% Q1 2026, per Panel 5). Not a forecast.

## 3. Per-panel decks

One sentence each, italic, set in `body-sm` per the panel template. Each
deck answers "so what" for the panel and does not recite the chart's own
numbers.

### Panel 1 -- Headline CPI

> *Headline accelerated to 2.4% from 1.8% in February as the carbon-levy
> base effect unwound; the three-month annualized read of 2.9% is the
> diagnostic short-window measure on the underlying near-term pulse.*

### Panel 2 -- BoC preferred core measures

> *Core-trim and core-median have rolled from a 3.0-3.1% corridor in
> mid-2025 to 2.2-2.3% in March -- a re-anchoring close to target on the
> measures the Bank of Canada watches.*

### Panel 3 -- Breadth

> *The 2.4% headline obscures unusually wide dispersion: a large share
> of the basket is running well below 1%, another sizable share is
> running above 3%, and the middle band is the smallest of the three.*

### Panel 4 -- Sub-aggregates

> *Shelter has cooled below the headline as mortgage-interest cost has
> faded; energy and food are now the marginal pressure-points, carrying
> the imprint of the Middle East energy shock through fuel and
> transport.*

### Panel 5 -- Inflation expectations

> *Firms have re-anchored -- only 11% of Business Outlook Survey
> respondents now expect inflation above 3% over the next two years,
> down from 84% in late 2022 -- while consumer one-year expectations
> remain stuck just below 4%.*

### Panel 6 -- Pass-through watch

> *USDCAD is roughly flat year-over-year, leaving tradable-goods CPI
> with little exchange-rate impulse; composition-adjusted wages at 3.1%
> Y/Y run close to non-shelter services prices, neither leading the
> other cleanly.*

## 6. Methodology note stubs

Two-sentence stubs only; researcher fleshes out the full methodology
drawer content per `basics-layer-template.md` Section 7. The voice is
plain methodology, not editorial; the stubs name the construction and
the load-bearing assumption.

### Breadth -- continuous |tilt| percentile ladder (Panel 3)

> Breadth is the weighted share of the CPI basket whose 12-month percent
> change falls into three Y/Y bins (below 1%, 1-3%, above 3%), computed
> on the 60 published component indexes (NSA, StatCan Table 18-10-0004-01)
> weighted by 2024 basket weights (Table 18-10-0007-01, applied from
> January 2025; mapping in `data/cpi_breadth_mapping.json`). The
> continuous |tilt| measure -- the absolute difference between the share
> above 3% and the share below 1% -- is ranked against its historical
> percentile distribution from 1996 forward to characterize how
> two-tailed the current dispersion is; the four state-name vocabulary
> (broad-based pressure, broad-based softening, clustered near target,
> polarized) remains available where the data clearly matches one, never
> as a forced classification.

[FLAG for researcher: the historical baseline file is in the boc-tracker
analyses directory (`analyses/inflation_distribution.csv`) per W3-R2
Panel 3 evidence section. Confirm the percentile-ladder cutoffs are
copied into the project's `data/` directory before the methodology stub
fires; the current tilt of -16.8pp (27.2% - 44.0%) deserves a percentile
stamp before it goes into the methodology drawer.]

### Pass-through watch (Panel 6)

> Pass-through is shown as two side-by-side strip charts: USDCAD Y/Y
> lagged 12 months overlaid with goods-excluding-energy Y/Y (the
> textbook tradable-goods pass-through window per BoC SWP-2014-13 and
> SDP-2019-11), and LFS-Micro composition-adjusted wage Y/Y overlaid
> contemporaneously with services-excluding-shelter Y/Y. No regression
> is fit; the panel is a visual diagnostic, and Canadian pass-through
> coefficients on both channels have been unstable since the mid-2010s.

### Headline CPI and core measures (Panels 1, 2)

> Headline CPI Y/Y is StatCan's not-seasonally-adjusted all-items index
> (Table 18-10-0004-01, V41690973), computed as the 12-month percent
> change. M/M is on the SA index (Table 18-10-0006-01, V41690914). The
> three-month annualized read is the three-month log-change of the SA
> index, annualized geometrically: ((index_t / index_{t-3}) ^ 4) - 1.
> Core-trim and core-median are the Bank of Canada's published Y/Y
> measures; common is shown as a hover footnote and has been
> deprioritized in BoC policy commentary since late 2022. Consensus is
> the median of available bank forecasts at the time of the print, with
> the panel composition stamped in the data layer; the BoC MPR central
> projection is the fallback when consensus is unavailable.

### Sub-aggregate derivations (Panel 4)

> Services excluding shelter and goods excluding energy are derived
> aggregates, constructed as the weighted residual of the all-services
> index less the shelter index, and the all-goods index less the energy
> index, using major-aggregate basket weights from StatCan Table
> 18-10-0007-01 (2024 basket, applied from January 2025; next basket
> update due 2030). The construction is reproducible from the
> `analyses/` script; basket-vintage and weight source are stamped on
> each derived value. The construction matches the BoC's published
> approach in its core-measures research papers.

### Inflation expectations (Panel 5)

> CSCE is the BoC's quarterly Canadian Survey of Consumer Expectations;
> the one-year and five-year values reported here are the survey's
> mean. BOS is the quarterly Business Outlook Survey; the
> "firms expecting CPI above 3% over the next two years" series is the
> ABOVE3 measure, and the four-bucket distribution (below 1%, 1-2%,
> 2-3%, above 3%) sums to 100% by construction. Q1 2026 was collected
> in late February and early March 2026 with a special supplement March
> 26 to April 2 on the Middle East war; the trajectory predates the
> supplement window.
