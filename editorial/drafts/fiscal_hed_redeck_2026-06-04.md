# Fiscal hed redeck — 2026-06-04

Surface: the six fiscal plate TITLES only (heds). Blurbs unchanged (gated).
Voice: writing-style.md §4.2 — sentence case, terminal period, one declarative
verb, names the finding, ≤14 words, one extending clause OK.

Direction (Jay, verbatim): heds aren't good; 01 should be plan-framing ("Ottawa
plans to balance the operating books, using the budget deficit for capital");
02 should be PBO-focused ("the budget watchdog says the government is doing X,
can't verify"); too many heds say "Ottawa."

Constraints applied:
- Subject-protagonist rule: each hed's grammatical subject = its beat's
  protagonist (01 plan, 02 watchdog, 03 deficit/spending, 04 ratio, 05 bill,
  06 bond program). "Ottawa"/"the government" as subject appears once in the
  deck (hed 01), per the cap.
- Mechanic fix on 01: capital BORROWING creates the deficit; the deficit does
  not fund capital. The plan is to balance operations and borrow only for
  capital — that borrowing is what keeps the total in deficit.
- 01 plan-framing, not achievement: "plans to" / "would," not "is balancing."
- 02 watchdog-as-subject, can't-verify framing. "Budget watchdog" used as the
  plain-language gloss on first deck appearance (PBO is established usage; the
  watchdog framing is already live across the fiscal drafts and dashboard_purpose).
- 2→3 seam fixed: 02 ends on "can't verify the anchor"; 03 opens on what the
  data CAN show plainly — the deficit narrows, and it does so from spending.
  The pivot from unverifiable-claim to verifiable-mechanism is the seam's repair.
- No new facts. Every claim reuses the gate-verified deck.

## One-line rationale, each

- **01** — Subject = "Ottawa" (the one allowed use; this is the plan beat, and
  the plan is the government's). "plans to" makes it plan-framing, not
  achievement; "is balancing" is cut. The mechanic is now correct: balance
  day-to-day spending, borrow only for capital — the borrowing is what leaves
  the total in deficit, so nothing here says "the deficit funds capital." 13
  words. Names the finding (the two-track design), not the chart's levels.

- **02** — Subject = "the budget watchdog" (the PBO is the protagonist of this
  beat). Says/can't-verify framing per direction: the watchdog "can't confirm,"
  and the reason is named — Ottawa's "own undisclosed definition of capital."
  Carries the chart's argument (two definitions, balance turns on which one) in
  the title. 14 words (at the cap). "Ottawa's" here is a possessive modifier,
  not the grammatical subject — the subject is the watchdog, satisfying the rule.

- **03** — Unchanged from the live deck. Subject = "the deficit" (the spending
  story is the protagonist). It already obeys the rule and names the finding
  (the narrowing is a spending story, revenue is flat). Kept verbatim because it
  is the hinge that repairs the 2→3 seam: after 02's "can't verify," 03 states
  plainly what the data does show. 10 words.

- **04** — Subject = "the debt ratio" (the ratio is the protagonist; the old hed
  led with "Ottawa and the PBO"). Names the agreed finding — flat, and the
  watchdog agrees — which is exactly the beat (this is the part NOT in dispute).
  "the watchdog agrees" echoes 02's watchdog without repeating "PBO," and sets
  up the turn into 05. 11 words.

- **05** — Subject = "the ratio" → pivots to "the bill" (the bill is the
  protagonist this plate isolates). Jay's "bill to carry it" framing. The
  extending clause off the comma is the canon-permitted single extension. Names
  the divergence the plate exists to show: flat ratio, rising carrying cost. The
  "ratio holds flat" half deliberately repeats 04's verb to make the turn audible
  in succession. 13 words.

- **06** — Unchanged from the live deck. Subject = "the bond program" (the
  protagonist; the take is that the record is a duration story, not a bill
  story). Already obeys the rule, names the finding, carries the one number that
  IS the takeaway (a record). 14 words.

## Heds-in-succession read-through (proof the sequence tells the story)

Reading only the six titles, in order: Ottawa plans to balance day-to-day
spending and borrow only for capital. The budget watchdog can't confirm the
operating books balance under Ottawa's own undisclosed definition of capital.
The deficit narrows from the spending side; revenue holds. The debt ratio holds
flat, and the watchdog agrees it does. The ratio holds flat, but the bill to
carry the debt keeps rising. The bond program, not bills, drives issuance to a
record near $612 billion.

The arc lands: a plan (balance operations, borrow for capital) → a watchdog who
can't verify the plan, because the definition that makes it work is Ottawa's own
and undisclosed → what the data CAN show plainly, that the deficit narrows on the
spending side → the one thing both sides agree on, a flat debt ratio → but flat
doesn't mean costless: the bill to carry the debt keeps rising → and the market
absorbs that debt as record bond issuance, duration not bills. The watchdog
appears in 02 (can't verify) and again in 04 (agrees) — the deliberate contrast
that the section is built on: dispute over the operating books, agreement on the
ratio. The "Ottawa" subject fires once, in 01, where the plan is genuinely the
government's; everywhere else the protagonist is the thing the chart measures.

The repaired seam is 2→3: 02 closes on "can't confirm," 03 opens on a flat,
plainly-true mechanism. The reader moves from "the contested claim" to "here is
what isn't contested" without a dead beat between them.
