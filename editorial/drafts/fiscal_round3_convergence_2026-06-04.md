## The forecast-language rule (house standard authored this pass)

A forward-looking outcome must never appear in bare present indicative as if it
were observed fact. Acceptable markers:

- **(a) attribution** — "Ottawa's plan…", "the watchdog projects…", "the forecast
  closes…", "on the government's plan", "is projected to".
- **(b) set-to constructions** — "is set to", "is on track to" (only when the source
  itself projects the outcome).
- **(c) explicit horizon framing that SIGNALS projection** — "by 2030-31" alone is
  NOT sufficient; a year-stamp is not a marker. It must be paired with (a) or (b),
  or read inside a clause already marked as forecast.

Observed/actual data keeps the bare indicative. The dividing line is the data
vintage: a printed Fiscal Monitor figure is indicative; a Spring Economic Update
projection or a PBO outlook endpoint is attributed.
