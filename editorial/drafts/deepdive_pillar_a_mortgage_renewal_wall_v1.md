## 2. Lede

Three paragraphs. State the question, state the answer, name the
channel that still matters. The 7am Bay Street allocator should leave
the lede with a sharper view; if the only thing they take is the title
and the lede, that is enough.

> The mortgage renewal wall has peaked. The fixed-rate stock booked at
> 2020-21 contract rates -- the cohort that has carried the bulk of the
> renewal-payment-shock distribution -- has now mostly rolled, and the
> mechanical pulse on household cash flows has begun to fade. Mortgage
> interest cost in the CPI tells the story most cleanly: at 0.28% Y/Y
> in March 2026, MIC's contribution to inflation is essentially
> extinguished, after running near double digits at the 2023-2024
> peak. The Bank of Canada cut to 2.75% on April 29, 2026, which puts
> a further floor under the residual.
>
> But peaked is not over. Roughly **TK** of the residential mortgage
> stock remains to renew through the end of 2027, and the 2026H2 and
> 2027 cohorts roll at average contract rates that are still meaningfully
> above what they replaced. The shape of the distribution has changed
> more than the size: payment shocks are now concentrated in a smaller
> tail of borrowers with longer original amortizations and weaker
> equity buffers, and the regional dispersion of stress -- Toronto and
> Vancouver leading -- is wider than the national aggregate suggests.
> Arrears are still low by Canadian historical standards and still
> moving in the wrong direction.
>
> The piece that follows argues three things. First, that the *count*
> of renewers facing a step-up in payment has peaked, but the *severity*
> in the remaining tail is the variable to watch through 2027. Second,
> that the consumer-side response has already shifted from "spread the
> shock through extended amortizations" to "absorb the shock through
> lower discretionary consumption" -- a slower-burn channel that does
> not show up cleanly in any single monthly print. Third, that the
> bank-side residual is now a regional-credit story, not a national
> one. We name what would change our mind in Section 7.

## 4. Section II -- The stress

Payment shock math.

> Payment shock at renewal is the cohort-average change in scheduled
> monthly mortgage payment when a contract resets from its original
> contract rate to the rate prevailing at renewal, holding amortization
> constant. The BoC's chartpack frames this as a distribution rather
> than a single number, for the right reason: the median renewer's
> experience differs sharply from the tail's. Through 2023 and 2024
> the median 2024-2025 cohort renewer faced a step-up of **roughly
> TK%** to **TK%** in scheduled monthly payment [TK5]; that median
> has now compressed for the 2026H1 and 2026H2 cohorts because
> renewal rates have fallen meaningfully from the late-2023 peak as
> the BoC has cut.
>
> The distribution is the read that matters. The bulk of the
> renewal-payment-shock mass that dominated 2024 commentary --
> step-ups in the 30 to 40 percent range -- has cleared. What
> remains is a smaller tail with three characteristics: original
> amortizations at or near 30 years (a feature, not a fluke, of
> 2020-21 originations in expensive markets), uninsured loan-to-value
> ratios that leave little equity room to refinance into a
> longer-amortization product without re-qualification under
> tighter B-20 stress-test conventions, and concentration in the
> Greater Toronto and Greater Vancouver CMAs where the original
> nominal loan sizes were largest. The OSFI Bank Financial Data
> uninsured-residential line carries this tail as roughly **TK%**
> of the chartered-bank residential book [TK6].
>

> cohorts. Histogram by cohort showing share of renewers facing

## 5. Section III -- The response

Household side: extensions, consumption response, savings rate.
Bank side: PCL, arrears, exposure.

> The consumer-side response has rotated. In 2023-2024, the dominant
> margin of adjustment was structural: lenders extended amortizations,
> in some cases past the contractual maximum at the cost of a future
> re-amortization at re-qualification, to spread the renewal shock
> across a longer schedule. OSFI's Bank Financial Data showed the
> share of the chartered-bank book at amortizations beyond 30 years
> rising materially through that window. That margin has now largely
> been used: borrowers who could extend have, and the post-rate-cut
> environment has reduced the incremental pressure to extend further.
>
> The current margin of adjustment is discretionary consumption. The
> StatCan quarterly expenditure cut shows household consumption at
> 0.4% Q/Q in Q4 2025 (2.3% on the year), with the household saving
> rate at 4.4% in Q4 2025 and 4.9% for 2025 annual. Both numbers
> sit inside the post-COVID range and tell the same story: aggregate
> consumption is positive but soft, and the saving rate is normalizing
> downward from the 2023-2024 highs as households absorb the
> cash-flow imprint of higher debt service. None of these aggregates
> are dramatic. The renewal channel does not show up as a single
> month's collapse in retail sales; it shows up as a multi-quarter
> drag on the discretionary-services side, partly offsetting the
> mechanical fade of MIC in the inflation print.
>
> The bank-side residual is now a regional credit story, not a
> national one. CMHC's Residential Mortgage Industry Report places
> the headline mortgage-arrears rate at **TK%** as of the most
> recent quarterly vintage [TK9], still well below the post-2008
> peak and well below comparable readings in other advanced
> economies, but moving higher quarter-over-quarter. CBA's
> chartered-bank-only arrears series, which prints monthly and runs
> 30-90 days behind RMIR, shows the same direction at a higher
> frequency [TK10 on current value]. Big-Six provisions for credit
> losses on residential mortgages have built through the 2024-2025
> renewal window from a low base; the build is concentrated in the
> uninsured Ontario and BC portfolios per the most recent earnings
> supplements [TK11 on specific PCL numbers].
>

## 6. Section IV -- The tail

What is still to come: 2026H2 and 2027 cohorts.

> The tail of the renewal wall is structurally smaller than its
> peak but mechanically slower to clear. The 2026H2 cohort renews
> against an average policy rate that is roughly **TK basis points**
> [TK14] lower than the cohort that renewed in 2024H2, which is
> the cleanest comparator vintage. The 2027 calendar year cohort
> rolls at rates that are closer still to what they were originally
> booked at, because the 2022 originations were already partly
> repriced into the BoC's hiking cycle. The remaining renewal-rate
> spread for the 2026H2 and 2027 cohorts combined is materially
> narrower than the spread the 2024-2025 cohorts faced.
>
> Three caveats sit on top of that mechanical read. First, the BoC
> policy path from here is the marginal input. The April 2026 cut
> to 2.75% lowered the renewal-rate ceiling for the residual
> cohorts; a pause-and-hold path from here delivers a different
> distribution than a continued-cut path. The Bank's most recent
> Monetary Policy Report [vintage TK -- April 2026] frames the
> central path; deviations from that path remap the residual
> distribution non-linearly because the renewal-payment-shock math
> is convex in the renewal-rate spread.
>
> Second, the 2022-vintage variable-rate cohort is the wildcard.
> Variable-rate borrowers who hit their trigger rate during 2023-2024
> and entered negative-amortization territory have a re-amortization
> obligation at renewal that is structurally different from the
> fixed-rate cohort's clean step-up. Per the BoC chartpack family
> [vintage TK], the variable-rate share of the residual stack is
> roughly **TK%** [TK15], and the share of that share that hit a
> trigger rate is roughly **TK%** [TK16]. These borrowers face a
> step-up in scheduled payment that is mechanically larger than
> the fixed-rate cohort step-up at the same renewal rate, because
> they are also re-amortizing.
>
> Third, the regional dispersion widens through the tail. The
> Greater Toronto and Greater Vancouver concentration of the 2020-21
> origination cohorts means the 2026H2 and 2027 tail is geographically
> concentrated in two CMAs whose price trajectories have themselves
> turned. CREA MLS HPI showed national benchmark prices down 1.4%
> Y/Y to April 2026, with Toronto and Vancouver leading the national
> turn directionally [TK17 on the per-CMA Y/Y values]. Negative
> price momentum interacts with renewal stress through the
> uninsured-LTV channel: borrowers whose original LTV was tight
> have less room to refinance into a different product, and the
> set of available products narrows further when the property's
> mark-to-market value has fallen.
>
> [INLINE CHART PLACEHOLDER -- Section IV anchor chart]
>
> *Chart: Mortgage renewal cohort schedule from 2026Q3 forward,
> by quarter through 2027Q4. Bars: outstanding balance ($ billions)
> by renewal quarter, decomposed into fixed-rate (light) and
> variable-rate (dark). Overlaid line (right axis): average
> estimated renewal-rate spread vs original contract rate (basis
> points).*
> *Source: BoC Residential Mortgage Market chartpack [vintage TK];
> OSFI Bank Financial Data M4 [vintage TK]. Author calculations.*
> *Annotation: "Tail cohorts: smaller in count, longer in
> resolution."* [annotation copy TK on verified numbers]

Voice notes:
- The "convex in the renewal-rate spread" line is editorial
  judgment grounded in the standard mortgage-payment math
  (the payment-shock derivative with respect to renewal rate
  is monotonically increasing). The piece does not derive it
  on the page; the methodology footnote on the chart can carry
  the formal note if fact-checker asks.
- The "regional dispersion widens through the tail" thesis is
  the sharper view this piece takes against the consensus
  framing of "the wall has cleared." It is the central
  divergence call; Section 7 names the falsification trigger.

## 7. What would change our view

> Three triggers fire against this view.
>
> **The wall peaks earlier than this piece claims if**: CMHC's
> mortgage arrears rate (90+ days) stalls or rolls over within the
> next two quarterly RMIR releases [next vintage TK]; CBA's
> chartered-bank monthly arrears series follows suit within four
> monthly prints; and the Big-Six provisions for credit losses on
> residential mortgages stabilize or decline in the next earnings
> cycle [Q2 2026 reports, mid-2026]. The combination would say the
> 2024-2025 stress has now begun to dissipate faster than the
> cohort-mechanics model predicts; the tail is shorter, not just
> shallower.
>
> **The wall extends past 2027 if**: the BoC's terminal cut is
> shallower than the April 2026 MPR central path implies, which
> would keep the 2026H2 and 2027 renewal-rate spread wider than
> the mechanical fade described in Section IV; CMHC arrears
> continue to rise through 2026H2 and into 2027 without inflecting;
> or the Toronto and Vancouver MLS HPI Y/Y deltas push more
> negative through 2026H2 (a roughly **-5% Y/Y or worse** read by
> the December 2026 vintage [TK18 on the cleaner threshold]),
> which would meaningfully widen the share of the residual tail
> that is constrained by negative-equity dynamics rather than by
> cash-flow stress.
>
> **We are wrong about the regional-credit framing if**: the
> next two CMHC RMIR vintages show arrears building most rapidly
> outside Ontario and BC -- specifically, if Alberta's CMHC
> arrears rate moves above ON or BC in the next vintage, the
> regional-credit story is energy-cycle-driven, not
> renewal-cohort-driven, and the renewal wall is no longer the
> right frame for thinking about Canadian residential credit
> stress through 2027.
>
> The honest answer on the count of renewers is "peaked." The
> honest answer on the depth of the residual tail is "narrower
> than the count suggests, deeper than the aggregates show, and
> regional." The cheapest test that would resolve the regional
> framing is the next CMHC RMIR vintage [release date TK], which
> we will mark against here on release.

Voice notes:
- Falsification triggers per the Mode B closing discipline
  in `writing-style.md` Section 7. Each trigger is dated and
  testable. The closing paragraph is the call and the
  watchpoint, not a summary.
