## Section abstract (80-120 words)

Page header question: **"What is Canada's fiscal stance, and is it consistent with the cycle?"**

Modestly expansionary on the federal side, with the live argument over what counts as capital and what counts as operating. The deficit is tracking near 2% of GDP and debt-to-GDP is holding broadly flat, the trajectory the IMF reads as counter-cyclical and the watchdog reads as constrained by debt service eating an ever-larger share of revenues. Provincial stances diverge — Ontario and Quebec carrying mid-30s debt-to-GDP, Alberta and BC well below — and so does the federal scorekeeping itself, with the Parliamentary Budget Officer reclassifying tens of billions of the government's "capital" spend back into operating.

## Plate 1 — Federal trajectory

**Title:** The federal track is the same cadence as recent years, not a counter-cyclical lean.

**Blurb:**
The deficit is on course to land near 2.1% of GDP for fiscal 2025-26, the trajectory the Spring Economic Update projects and the path the Fiscal Monitor has been printing into. Eleven months in, the year-to-date balance sits at a $25.5 billion deficit, after a $5.7 billion February surplus pulled the cumulative line off its January trough. Heavy autumn outflows and tax-receipt months tugging the YTD reading back is the standard fiscal shape — not a fiscal stance leaning against the cycle.

## Plate 2 — Debt service / revenues

**Title:** Debt service is consuming a rising share of every federal revenue dollar.

**Blurb:**
Roughly ten cents of every dollar of federal revenue now goes to servicing the debt, and the Parliamentary Budget Officer's projection has that share climbing past thirteen cents by 2030-31. The level itself is unremarkable by historical standards; the trajectory isn't. A 2022-onward structural step-up in carrying costs, compounding against a revenue base that grows with the economy rather than with rates, is what shrinks the room for fiscal response to the next downturn.

## Plate 3 — PBO vs DoF baseline delta

**Title:** The watchdog and the government no longer see the same deficit track.

**Blurb:**
The Department of Finance's most recent baseline projects a $66.9 billion deficit for fiscal 2025-26, declining to $53.2 billion by 2030-31; the Parliamentary Budget Officer's September 2025 outlook runs [X] billion wider over the same horizon. The reader who anchors on only one of them is reading half the picture.

## Plate 4 — Provincial debt-to-GDP

**Title:** Provincial fiscal stance does not generalize: the spread runs from Alberta's 10.5% to Quebec's 38.8%.

**Blurb:**
Ontario at 37.7% and Quebec at 38.8% carry the largest sub-sovereign debt loads in the country relative to the size of their economies; Alberta at 10.5% sits at the other end, with British Columbia at 30.6% on a taxpayer-supported basis. The dispersion is not noise — resource-revenue dependency, urban tax-base concentration, and a decade of differing fiscal restraint each pull a province into its own track. The federal fiscal stance is not the country's fiscal stance, and any read of Canadian fiscal policy that stops at Ottawa is missing the half that funds health, education, and most capital spending.
