**The true take.** Revenue holds in a tight band the whole way (15.5%–16.6% of GDP
across every year shown); it is program spending that rose into the deficit era and
program spending that the plan pulls back. By the forecast endpoint, spending is
restored to its pre-pandemic position *below* revenue. Note: program expenses exclude
public debt charges, so revenue sitting above program spending is **not** a surplus —
it does not cover debt-service cost. The interpretation does not imply one.

**New title** (terminal period, one verb, names the finding — 12 words):

> Revenue barely moves; the deficit narrows because spending is pulled back to meet it.

> band near 16% of GDP throughout — through the pandemic, which drove program spending

> the gap entirely from the spending side, returning program expenses below revenue by
> decade's end and restoring the relationship that held for the thirty years before 2019.

**One-line rationale.** The verified series shows revenue flat and above program
spending for three decades; the deficit since 2019 is a spending-level move that the
plan reverses on the spending side, with revenue never asked to rise — the sharpest
claim the data supports, and the exact inverse of the failed round-1 take.
