## The intuition-with-rigor principle

> **"I need the intuition even if I want the rigorous work to be done."** — Jay, 2026-05-26

Every Sibley analytical product ships rigor AND intuition together. Not dumbed-down work — dumbed-down EXPLANATION of rigorous work.

The principle in plain form: if a smart non-expert can't follow the intuition behind a Sibley finding, the finding doesn't ship — even if the underlying methodology is correct.

## Why this is the standard

1. **It IS part of Sibley's edge.** The opaque competitors (BCA, Capital Economics, MRB) ship rigor without intuition. They sell on credentials. Sibley ships rigor WITH intuition — sells on transparency. Subscribers pay for the intuition layer almost as much as for the rigor itself, because intuition is what lets them DEFEND using Sibley's work internally.

2. **It's how trust is built without prior credentialing.** Sibley is a new shop. Buyers can't rely on a long track record. The way they can trust the work is by being able to follow it themselves — to spot-check it against their own judgment.

3. **It's how the work survives the cut-first test.** Wojtek's air/water/food frame: subscribers who can articulate WHY they trust Sibley's work to their boss don't cut the subscription when budgets tighten. Subscribers who took the work on faith do.

## Three places this applies

### 1. Quantitative products

Example: the USDCAD Score.

The work is methodologically rigorous (filter → embedded → purged walk-forward CV → Deflated Sharpe Ratio).

The deliverable includes a transparent diagnostic companion:
- Variable universe shown
- Selection stage outcomes visualized
- Backtest performance broken down by regime
- Honest failure-mode disclosure
- Plain-English captions on every chart and statistic
- Smart non-expert can follow the methodology choices in 15-20 minutes

If the diagnostic companion can't be read by a smart non-expert in a sitting, it ships as a redraft.

### 2. Research pieces

Example: the Build-Big trades-gap piece.

The work is rigorously sourced and methodologically defensible (BuildForce baseline + project-by-project labour estimates + named industry sources).

The deliverable explains the methodology in the piece itself — not buried in an appendix, but woven into the prose so a smart non-expert reader can follow exactly what work was done and why the conclusion is supported.

The methodology box at the foot is short (~200 words), plain-English, and reproducible.

### 3. Advisory engagements

Even when delivering work that an expert would call sophisticated, the client must walk away saying "I understand what Sibley found and why."

Sibley does not produce black-box advisory deliverables. The framework, the data, the reasoning chain are always visible.

## The test before any analytical work ships

Before any Sibley analytical deliverable goes out, the question to ask: **"Can a smart non-expert reader, in a single sitting, follow the intuition behind what we found and why?"**

If yes — ship.
If no — the prose / visuals / methodology disclosure needs another pass. The work itself isn't the problem; the legibility is.

## Why this isn't just a writing standard

The intuition-with-rigor principle affects HOW the analytical work itself is done, not just how it's reported.

- Methodology choices that can't be defended in plain English get questioned earlier.
- Variables / signals that are statistically significant but conceptually opaque get filtered earlier.
- Methods that don't produce interpretable outputs (e.g., black-box deep learning without SHAP or equivalent interpretability layer) are deprioritized.

The principle disciplines the work, not just the report.

## What this does NOT mean

This is NOT a license to oversimplify. Sibley's reputation is built on rigorous work. The intuition layer is layered ON TOP of rigor, not in place of it.

If a finding is genuinely complex and resists plain-English explanation, the answer is to do more methodology work to find a more interpretable angle — not to ship the complexity in opaque form.
